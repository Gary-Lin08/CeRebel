# Weightlifting Sport Pack — Disclaimer and Safe Use

## Not medical advice

CeRebel weightlifting content summarizes **open coaching literature, resistance-training consensus statements,
and biomechanics reviews**. It is **not** a substitute for licensed medical care, physical therapy diagnosis,
or individualized return-to-lifting protocols after injury.

- Sharp pain, joint locking, radiating numbness, or chest pain during lifting require stopping the set and
  seeking qualified care — do not rely on an app finding to continue max-effort attempts.
- Spinal and shoulder symptoms during squats, deadlifts, or overhead work need in-person evaluation; video
  cannot rule out disc, labral, or vascular issues.

## Not a programming or load prescription tool

This pack describes **movement quality and common technique patterns**. It does **not**:

- Calculate one-rep max, prescribe working percentages, or replace certified strength coaches.
- Approve attempting maximal loads based on a single good-looking warm-up rep.
- Enforce competition depth rules (powerlifting vs Olympic vs gym standards) without explicit user context.

Always use spotters, safety bars, and collars appropriate to your facility. Bail procedures (squat) and
drop policies (deadlift) are gym-specific — not encoded here.

## Body tracking limitations

Single-camera pose estimation can mis-estimate:

- Lumbar flexion when the torso is foreshortened or clothed loosely.
- Knee valgus in pure frontal view without 3D or dual-camera fusion.
- Bar path relative to feet when the bar is occluded or not tracked.
- Hip hinge angle when the camera is too low or high.

Findings describe **visible tendencies**, not force-plate or EMG ground truth. Lower confidence when joints
are occluded, frame rate is below ~30 fps for fast concentric phases, or the lifter wears baggy clothing.

## Overhead press specific risks

Strict pressing overhead can aggravate shoulder impingement symptoms in some individuals. CeRebel does not
screen for shoulder pathology. Users with prior shoulder surgery or chronic pain should work with a clinician
or coach before emulating generic lockout positions.

## Youth and novice lifters

Children and adolescents need age-appropriate supervision, technique emphasis, and conservative loading per
NGB and clinical consensus. Do not tell youth to match adult depth, load, or bar speed from elite examples
in social media.

## No guaranteed performance outcomes

Technique cues may support efficient patterns discussed in research, but **strength gains depend on program
design, sleep, nutrition, training age, and individual anatomy**. Do not promise PR outcomes from one drill.

## Coaching relationship

Automated briefs support athletes and coaches who already have a training plan. They do not replace hands-on
coaching for learning bracing, breathing, and rack/unrack safety.

## Emergency

For acute injury in the weight room, follow facility emergency procedures. Suspected spinal cord or head
injury: do not move the person; call emergency services.

## Product behavior

- Weightlifting pack features may be gated as **coming_soon** until detector validation completes.
- Absence of a finding is not proof of safe loading or injury-free mechanics.
- Mislabeled clips (e.g., golf swing analyzed as squat) should be refused when cross-sport detectors ship.

## User-facing short disclaimer (for UI copy)

“Movement tips from video — not medical advice or load prescription. Stop if you feel pain. Use proper safety equipment.”
