# Weightlifting Biomechanics — Overview (open literature summaries)
# authority: B+ unless noted | NOT medical advice | body-first, bar optional

================================================================================
SCOPE
================================================================================

This document summarizes peer-reviewed and consensus literature on **back squat**, **conventional deadlift**,
and **standing overhead press** mechanics relevant to pose-based coaching. Claims are relationships and trends,
not individualized load prescriptions.

Primary segments modeled from video:
- Pelvis / hip center, femur (hip–knee), shank (knee–ankle), thorax, upper arm, forearm/wrist proxy for bar.

================================================================================
COORDINATE & ANGLE CONVENTIONS (product alignment)
================================================================================

- Hip angle: trunk–thigh angle in sagittal plane (flexion decreases in deep squat/hinge).
- Knee flexion: increasing angle = more flexed in standard biomechanics convention (match OpenSim pack).
- Trunk angle: thorax vs vertical; forward lean = positive pitch in sagittal side view.
- Lumbar proxy: thorax–pelvis relative angle — noisy in 2D; use qualitative trends only.

Always downgrade confidence when camera not sagittal/oblique.

================================================================================
SQUAT — KINETIC & KINEMATIC THEMES
================================================================================

Joint moments:
- Squat loads hip, knee, and ankle extensors; relative contribution shifts with depth, stance width, and load.
- Deeper squats generally increase knee extensor demand at bottom; hip extensor demand remains substantial at heavy loads.

Trunk angle & bar position (appearance):
- Low-bar vs high-bar differences increase hip hinge and trunk lean for a given depth — body-only video cannot
  read bar position; infer cautiously from elbow position and wrist height if bar tracked.

Knee valgus:
- Frontal-plane knee motion correlates with valgus moments in some studies; inter-individual anatomy and cueing
  affect outcome. Product flags visible medial knee drift, not ACL risk scores.

Depth:
- "Parallel" and "below parallel" definitions vary by federation and anthropometry (hip vs knee landmark).
- Product uses hip vs knee height proxy — users should confirm intent with coach.

Tempo & reversal:
- Rapid eccentric and bounce at bottom increase rate of force development demands; pause squats reduce elastic rebound.

Youth:
- Growth, limb ratios, and supervision dominate safety literature over absolute depth numbers (A consensus).

Sources (representative):
- Escamilla RF et al. — squat biomechanics reviews
- Schoenfeld BJ et al. — squat depth and muscle activation meta-analyses
- NSCA / ACSM resistance training position statements (A)

================================================================================
DEADLIFT — HINGE MECHANICS
================================================================================

First pull:
- Successful pulls coordinate knee extension and hip extension; early hip rise without shoulder rise shifts
  mechanics toward stiff-leg patterns and often increases lumbar flexion moment if trunk rounds.

Bar path:
- Bar should stay close to body to minimize moment arm on lumbar spine and hips; forward drift visible via wrist–foot
  relationship when bar not tracked.

Trunk stiffness:
- Spinal stability literature emphasizes bracing and co-contraction; visible rounding is a technique/coaching target,
  not a diagnosis of tissue failure from one rep on video.

Sumo vs conventional:
- Sumo widens base, changes hip/knee moments; v1 pack checkpoints emphasize conventional — sumo shares hinge themes
  but different knee tracking.

Lockout:
- Completed by hip extension (glute max) with neutral-ish ribs; excessive lumbar hyperextension is a distinct pattern
  from hip-driven lockout in coaching models.

Sources (representative):
- Cholewicki J, McGill SM — lifting biomechanics
- Hales ME et al. — deadlift kinematics
- Swinton PA et al. — biomechanical comparison of DL variants

================================================================================
OVERHEAD PRESS — UPPER EXTREMITY & TRUNK
================================================================================

Force line:
- Strict press requires shoulder flexion and elbow extension with stable scapulothoracic rhythm; excessive thorax
  extension shifts demand to lumbar extensors (lean-back press).

Bar path:
- Optimal path is near-vertical in side view with head translation to allow clearance — forward loop paths increase
  shoulder torque requirements in simplified models.

Leg drive:
- Push press and jerk patterns deliberately use knee/hip extension; strict press minimizes lower-body excursion —
  detectable via knee angle change before wrist peak.

Shoulder load:
- Overhead work loads rotator cuff and deltoid complex; impingement risk is individual and medical — literature
  supports scapular control and progressive loading, not universal "safe" angles from 2D pose.

Sources (representative):
- Saeterbakken AH, Fimland MS — shoulder press variants
- Kinetic analyses of standing overhead press (open access reviews)

================================================================================
GRF & IMU (optional future channels)
================================================================================

When force plates or IMU are unavailable (default product path):
- Infer effort from segment speeds and joint angular velocities only.
- Do not estimate bar load in kg from video.

================================================================================
MEASUREMENT LIMITATIONS (repeat for RAG)
================================================================================

- 2D pose underestimates axial rotation and true lumbar flexion.
- Clothing, belts, and plates occlude joints.
- Wrap-around cameras help knee valgus; side view helps trunk and bar path proxies.

See Docs/002_DISCLAIMER.txt.
