# Squat, Deadlift, OHP — Biomechanics Deep Dive (B+ summaries)
# Focus content for RAG retrieval on the three v1 lifts

================================================================================
BACK SQUAT — STANCE, DEPTH, AND SEGMENT COUPLING
================================================================================

Stance width:
- Wider stances increase hip adductor involvement and can alter trunk angle at same depth.
- Narrow stances may increase ankle dorsiflexion demand — heel lift fault more common with limited ankle ROM.
- Product: measure ankle–knee–hip only; do not infer muscle activation percentages without EMG channel.

Femur/torso ratios:
- Long femur lifters appear more forward-leaning at depth with upright chest cues — anthropometry interacts with
  "good morning" fault heuristics; avoid false positives on long-limbed athletes.

Ascent strategies:
- "Hip drive" vs "knee drive" coaching cues change observable knee–hip extension sequencing slightly but both can
  succeed when bar stays balanced over mid-foot proxy.

Safety literature (A/B+):
- Squats performed with supervision and progressive loading are supported in healthy populations in consensus statements.
- Pain during squatting warrants clinical evaluation — not continued loading based on app feedback.

Detectable fault signatures (pose):
- good_morning: thorax pitch rate >> hip flexion rate early descent.
- valgus: medial knee vs foot in frontal/oblique view.
- bounce: non-monotonic knee angle at bottom.

================================================================================
CONVENTIONAL DEADLIFT — START, PASS THE KNEE, LOCKOUT
================================================================================

Start position:
- Hip height sets knee angle and shoulder-over-bar relationship; "too squatty" increases knee forward travel and
  can reposition bar relative to shins.

Initial pull slack:
- Pre-movement shoulder depression and lat engagement reduce visible upper-back rounding; may appear as brief
  stillness before E_PULL_START.

Sticky points:
- Common sticking region near knee: knee extension may lag if bar forward or hips high.
- Hitched pulls show velocity stagnation — distinguish from intentional pause deadlifts via user tag.

Spine biomechanics (careful wording):
- Flexion under load increases tissue stress in biomechanical models; individual tolerance varies widely.
- Product language: "visible rounding pattern" not "herniated disc risk."

RDL vs deadlift:
- RDL emphasizes hip hinge with minimal knee bend; useful drill for fault_dl_hips_shoot and bar-close awareness.

Lockout errors:
- Hyperextension: thorax past vertical, ribs flared — distinct from neutral hip extension finish.
- Soft knees: knee flexion at lockout while hips appear extended.

================================================================================
STRICT OVERHEAD PRESS — PATH, CLEARANCE, STABILITY
================================================================================

Setup variants:
- Front rack: elbows forward, wrists extended; thoracic extension moderate.
- Too much layback before bar moves = fault_ohp_excessive_leanback signature.

Head movement:
- "Head through" at lockout is standard coaching — head translates forward under bar in side view; do not confuse
  with cervical flexion fault unless extreme and sustained.

Grip width:
- Wider grip reduces ROM, changes shoulder abduction; narrow grip increases elbow flare risk when mobility limited.

Scapular rhythm:
- Upward rotation and elevation occur through press; "no shrug" coaching targets excessive elevation at lockout only.

Push press contamination:
- Knee flexion–extension burst before wrist velocity peak is clearest strict vs push press discriminator for v1.

Seated vs standing:
- Seated removes leg drive — useful regression in drills; biomechanics reduce hip extension contribution to zero.

================================================================================
BILATERAL SYMMETRY
================================================================================

Squat: hip hike at bottom may reflect ankle mobility asymmetry, past injury habit, or bar uneven load (if bar tracked).

Deadlift: mixed grip is standard on heavy pulls but changes shoulder rotation appearance — note in capture metadata.

OHP: asymmetric wrist height at lockout — bar tilt or unilateral weakness proxy.
