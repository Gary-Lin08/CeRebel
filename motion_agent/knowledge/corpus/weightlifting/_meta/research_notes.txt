0. SCOPE & EVIDENCE USE
================================================================================

- Pack covers: back/front/overhead squat patterns, conventional/sumo deadlift, and
  Olympic-style snatch / clean & jerk progressions used in S&C (body-first phases).
- Measurable on body: hip/knee/ankle flexion, trunk-tibia angle, lumbar neutrality,
  shoulder/elbow/wrist in rack and overhead catch, split vs squat receiving stance.
- Do NOT productize as medical load limits, 1RM prediction, or competition legality
  (platform rules) without A+ rule citations and disclaimer.
- Pooled lab cohorts (elite lifters, trained women, healthy adults) are not user-specific
  targets; phrase findings as "typical in published cohorts."
- Separate: powerlifting squat depth norms vs Olympic receiving squat vs clinical rehab squat.

1. FEDERATION — IWF (OLYMPIC WEIGHTLIFTING) A+
================================================================================

Authority A+ | License: © International Weightlifting Federation (TCRR); summarize only
URL (current TCRR PDF): https://iwf.sport/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2025/11/IWF-TCRR-2025-as-of-05-November-2025.pdf
Download hub: https://iwf.sport/downloads/

Body-relevant competition definitions (not automated officiating):
- Snatch: bar pulled in one motion to arms extended overhead; may recover from split or
  squat; bar may slide along thighs; feet only may touch platform until recovery.
- Clean & jerk: clean to shoulders/clavicles (not chest-before-stop); legs extended before
  jerk; jerk to locked arms overhead; hook grip permitted.
- "No lift" if bar does not pass knee height on unfinished attempt (2.4.2).
- Product phase labels can mirror: pull from floor → turnover → catch → recovery → overhead
  fixation (snatch/C&J) using visible joint events, not barbell weight.

Coaching materials hub (open federation research summaries) B+
URL: https://iwf.sport/coaching/
License: IWF-hosted reports; no explicit CC on PDFs — short summaries + URL; do not bulk
  republish PDF text in RAG chunks.

IWF scientific reports (barbell trajectory / success factors):
- 2018 WWC snatch kinematics (JWA/JISS): https://iwf.sport/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2026/01/2018-WWC_Scientific-Report-JAPAN.pdf
- 2019 JWC snatch kinematics: https://iwf.sport/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2026/01/2019-JWC_Scientific-Report-JAPAN.pdf
- 2020 success vs unsuccessful snatch (listed on coaching page; peer-reviewed twin):
  Nagao et al., Sports Biomechanics 2020; DOI https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2020.1787498
  PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32894607/

Key IWF/JISS report facts (2018–2019 snatch cohorts):
- 2D sagittal bar end tracked (50–60 Hz); plate diameter 0.45 m scale reference.
- Event labels A–F from bar height/velocity (e.g. max vertical velocity, max height).
- Top performers vs bottom 30%: superior lifters often catch lower relative to bar peak;
  backward bar displacement and timing differ by weight class in men’s snatch.
- Reports intended as training reference trajectories for developing lifters, not individual
  prescription.

Nagao 2020 (successful vs frontward drop failures, same weight attempts):
- Greater backward bar displacement turnover→catch (DxL) and peak backward bar velocity
  (pVx-) in successful lifts; excessive backward COM lean in turnover linked to failures.
- Maximum bar height (Dy1) did not differ between success and frontward drop failures in
  that elite male sample.

Additional reading links (IWF): https://iwf.sport/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2020/09/Coaching-additional-reading-links.pdf

USA Weightlifting (NGB, USOPC) B — certification content is paid/membership; public pages only
Level 1 overview: https://www.usaweightlifting.org/usaw-level-1-coaches-course
Public course descriptor PDF: https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blteb7d012fc7ebef7f/bltdde4067071cd768c/649cb1f705ac385f0eaec148/USAW_L1_Info_Packet_for_CEUs_2020.pdf

3. SQUAT BIOMECHANICS — PUBMED / PMC (AUTHORITY A / B+)
================================================================================

3A. Clinical commentary — modifiable squat parameters (trunk, tibia, stance, depth)
Straub & Powers, Int J Sports Phys Ther 2024 (PMC10987311)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10987311/
License: CC BY-NC 4.0 — non-commercial reuse with attribution; commercial RAG = summarize.
Body-first relationships (for fault cards, not single "correct" angles):
- Forward trunk ↑ hip flexion moment, ↓ knee flexion moment; upright trunk opposite.
- Forward trunk via lumbar flexion vs hip hinge changes spinal load tolerance.
- Forward tibia (dorsiflexion or heel lift) ↑ knee flexion moment / quadriceps demand.
- Toes out ~30° ↓ knee valgus moment in reported studies; wide stance ↑ frontal-plane
  knee/hip moments vs narrow in some protocols.
- Deep squat limited by hip flexion; posterior pelvic tilt at end range couples lumbar flexion.
- Trunk–tibia angle at peak knee flexion predicts hip- vs knee-extensor bias (Barrack et al.
  cited): ~10° thresholds for bias classification in that model.

3B. COM and joint coordination during parallel squat (healthy adults)
Kasahara et al., J Hum Kinet 2024 (PMC11307177) | CC BY 4.0
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11307177/
- Ankle reaches max dorsiflexion earliest; knee motion correlates with squat depth; hip/ankle
  relate to anteroposterior COM displacement in regression models.

3C. Squat depth vs sagittal timing (60 participants, bodyweight)
Zawadka et al., J Sports Sci Med 2020 (PMC7429430)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7429430/
License: PMC hosted; © journal — summarize with citation.
- Knee ROM most correlated with depth (r≈0.92); earlier ankle/pelvis timing associated with
  depth in that cohort.

3D. Method reference (overhead squat — markerless validity, not coaching norms)
Bae et al., PMC11603033 — useful for CeRebel measurement limits, not lift standards.

4. DEADLIFT BIOMECHANICS — PUBMED / PMC
================================================================================

4A. Conventional vs sumo (3D kinematics/kinetics/EMG, 85% 1RM, n=30 males)
Hanen et al., Front Bioeng Biotechnol 2025 (PMC12148905) | CC BY 4.0
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12148905/
- CDL: more hip flexion, ankle DF, hip extension moments, posterior-chain EMG emphasis.
- SDL: more frontal/transverse ROM and moments; more vastus lateralis in phase 1 in that study.
- Three events often used in literature: lift-off, bar passes knees, lockout upright.

4B. Deadlift vs good morning (sagittal moments, 25–50% BW loads)
Schellenberg et al., BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil 2013 (PMC3878967) | CC BY 2.0
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3878967/
- DL: large knee flexion; GM: minimal knee flexion, different hip/spine moment profile.

4C. Load effect on technique (strength-trained women, 70/90/100% 3RM)
Gundersen et al. (PMC12591051) | CC BY (PMC)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12591051/
- Maximal attempts: more thoracic flexion and erector spinae EMG vs submax; hip net joint
  moments lower at 70% vs 90–100% in that sample — caution linking max-load form to training loads.

4D. Olympic pulls and lumbar loading model (hang clean/snatch/deadlift context)
Rossi et al. / spine model with Olympic lifts (PMC4925752)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4925752/
- Reports peak L5 shear/compression/bending during pulls at submax loads in modeled cohort;
  PMC copyright — summarize; do not treat as injury thresholds for product.

4E. Heavy deadlift simulation (single subject musculoskeletal model)
Ramirez et al., Int J Numer Meth Biomed Eng 2023 (Wiley OA)
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/cnm.3680
- High L5-S1 compression/shear in one heavy trial — research model, not coaching prescription.

5. PHASE MODEL SEEDS (RECONCILE IN brain_plan / Phases/)
================================================================================

5A. Squat (general S&C / front squat for weightlifting)
Suggested visible events: setup → descent (hip/knee flex) → bottom (max knee flex or hip
limit) → ascent → lockout (hip/knee extension). Optional: trunk-tibia classification at bottom.

5B. Deadlift
Setup (hip hinge, neutral spine) → break floor → knee pass → lockout (hip/knee extended,
scapular set in literature).

5C. Power clean / snatch (NSCA + IWF aligned)
Start → first pull (torso angle stable) → transition (double knee bend) → second pull /
triple extension → pull-under → catch (front rack or overhead) → recovery.

5D. Clean & jerk (competition)
Clean phases as above → jerk dip → drive → split or squat under → fixation.

6. SAFETY & PRODUCT GUARDRAILS
================================================================================

- Acute back/knee pain, numbness, or loss of control → stop; licensed care (mirror Disclaimer).
- Do not infer bar load or spine force from monocular body IK; no "safe load" numbers in UI.
- Youth: use lighter progressions; no adult elite trajectory numbers for juniors.
- CC BY-NC sources (squat clinical review): attribute; avoid treating NC as commercial corpus paste.
