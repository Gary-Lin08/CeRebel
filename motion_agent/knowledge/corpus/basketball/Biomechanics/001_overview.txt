# Basketball Shooting Biomechanics — Overview (open literature summaries)
# Authority: B+ unless noted. Not medical advice. Jump shot + free throw focus.

================================================================================
1. WHY BODY-FIRST ANALYSIS WORKS WITHOUT THE BALL
================================================================================

Basketball shooting is a **whole-body kinetic chain**: ankles/knees/hips extend, trunk stabilizes or slightly extends,
shoulder elevates and elbow extends, wrist and fingers impart final velocity and orientation on the ball. Pose-based
pipelines anchor timing on **shooting-wrist speed peak** as a release proxy because ball release and wrist peak are
closely timed in controlled captures (B+). Without ball tracking, CeRebel still evaluates:

- Sequencing (legs before arms on jump shots)
- Set-point height and elbow–wrist relationship
- Guide-hand independence
- Landing absorption (jump shot)

================================================================================
2. JUMP SHOT VS FREE THROW — KEY DIFFERENCES
================================================================================

| aspect              | jump shot                          | free throw                          |
|---------------------|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| COM vertical motion | substantial rise before/at release | minimal; feet often stay planted    |
| knee strategy       | eccentric dip → explosive extension | optional quiet flex → extension     |
| time pressure       | higher (game speed)                 | self-paced routine                  |
| trunk               | may include slight lean/contest     | more repeatable upright templates   |
| landing             | bilateral absorption required       | n/a (no airborne phase)             |

Research comparing skilled and novice shooters often shows **greater lower-body contribution and more consistent
timing** in skilled jump shooters (B+). Free throw studies emphasize **routine consistency and reduced variability**
in joint angles across attempts (B+).

================================================================================
3. KINETIC CHAIN (QUALITATIVE SEQUENCE)
================================================================================

Classic teaching model (summarized from multiple kinematic papers, B+):

1. **Lower extremity**: ankle plantarflexion and knee/hip extension generate upward force.
2. **Trunk**: stabilizes and transfers force; excessive flexion or rotation can alter release path visible in 2D.
3. **Upper extremity**: shoulder elevation, elbow extension, wrist flexion/extension couple.
4. **Release window**: ball leaves hand near maximum wrist angular velocity; fingers last contact (not visible without ball).

For **jump shots**, leg extension typically **begins before** peak elbow/wrist extension in skilled groups.
**Arm-only** patterns (high wrist peak with delayed COM rise) appear more often in novices or fatigued states in
observational studies — product maps this to fault_arm_only_shot without claiming causality for injury.

For **free throws**, lower extremity work is subtler; performance correlates in some studies with **limited variability**
in elbow and knee angles shot-to-shot rather than maximal power (B+).

================================================================================
4. JOINT ANGLES — HOW TO TALK ABOUT NUMBERS IN PRODUCT
================================================================================

Published cohorts report **means and ranges** that differ by skill, gender, stature, and capture method (2D vs 3D).
Do **not** ship single-degree "ideal" targets as universal.

Useful qualitative patterns (B+):

- **Elbow flexion** decreases from set to release (extension trend); incomplete extension at wrist-speed peak → coaching
  focus on full reach, not a fixed elbow angle.
- **Knee flexion** at dip varies widely in jump shots; very low flexion may limit power; very deep flexion may delay release.
- **Shoulder abduction** ("flyaway elbow") varies by style; extreme outward elbow in frontal view is a common coaching fix
  for consistency, not a proven injury threshold in youth.

Youth: absolute angles smaller due to limb length; compare **within-athlete** repeatability rather than pro templates.

================================================================================
5. GROUND REACTION FORCES AND LANDING (JUMP SHOT)
================================================================================

Landing after shooting jumps produces ground reaction forces comparable to other small vertical jumps (B+). Coaching
and S&C literature recommends **knee flexion on landing** to dissipate energy (A/B+ for general landing education).
CeRebel flags stiff or asymmetric landings as **fault_stiff_landing** / **fault_asymmetric_landing** — population-level
biomechanics support soft bilateral landings; individual ACL risk cannot be scored from monocular pose alone.

================================================================================
6. OFF-HAND (GUIDE HAND) ROLE
================================================================================

The guide hand stabilizes the ball during lift; at release it should not impart forward force. Kinematic studies note
**asymmetric hand roles** — shooting hand high velocity, guide hand low (B+). Product uses off-hand wrist speed ratio
near release as a practical proxy (fault_guide_hand_push).

================================================================================
7. VISUAL ATTENTION AND HEAD STABILITY
================================================================================

Free throw and shooting studies link **gaze stability** and routine duration to performance variability (B+). Head jerk
at release (fault_head_jerk_release) is a low-severity coaching signal visible in pose, not a clinical diagnosis.

================================================================================
8. FATIGUE AND LOAD (TEAM CONTEXT)
================================================================================

Repeated jump shooting in practice increases cumulative jump load. Federation youth guidelines emphasize age-appropriate
practice duration and rest (B/A+ for policy). Biomechanics alone does not prescribe daily shot counts — see Training/001.

================================================================================
9. MEASUREMENT LIMITATIONS (REPEAT FOR RAG)
================================================================================

- 2D pose underestimates shoulder rotation and wrist release angle.
- "Release" event = wrist speed peak, not official ball release.
- Contested shots alter trunk lean — comparisons should tag context (open vs contested) when product supports tags.
