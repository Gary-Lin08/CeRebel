WABC Mini-Basketball Coaches Manual (ages ~5–12):
  URL: https://assets.fiba.basketball/image/upload/documents-corporate-wabc-coaching-manual-mini-basketball-eng.pdf
  Shooting-relevant themes:
  - Ages 5–7: exploration over analytical technique; lower rings (6–8 ft), size 3/5 balls; “throw high” cues
    before formal “lock and snap” / “high elbow”.
  - Ages 8–9: motor abilities still dominate; shooting introduced in fun, contested and uncontested games.
  - Ages 10–12: more analytical feedback but **no single perfect technique**; defenders early in learning
    preferred over isolated form-only drilling.
  - Rejects assumption of one ideal technique; successful elites show individual styles.

WABC Level 1 Coaches Course Manual (club-level juniors):
  URL: https://assets.fiba.basketball/image/upload/documents-corporate-wabc-coaching-level-manuals-level-1-eng.pdf
  Shooting-relevant themes (Offensive basketball skills §2.7):
  - Feedback timing: immediate feedback after attempts; praise correct process even on misses.
  - Cue words: e.g. “high elbow”, “elbow above the eye”, “lock and snap” for arm/wrist form.
  - Controlled success/failure: define good shot **process** vs outcome for youth confidence.
  - Work–rest: follow intensive drills with lower load (e.g. free throws).
  - Zone defence not recommended under ~15 in many federations — supports man-to-man skill development.

3. JUMP SHOT BIOMECHANICS — REVIEWS & PRIMARY STUDIES (AUTHORITY B+)
================================================================================

3A. Systematic review — youth jump shot performance (OPEN ACCESS, CC BY 4.0)
  França et al., Int J Environ Res Public Health, 2021.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18063283
  PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8005190/
  PDF: https://www.mdpi.com/2060-4601/18/6/3283/pdf
  Sample: 12 studies, players 10–19, organized youth basketball (excludes NBA/Euroleague-only samples).
  Game-related modifiers emphasized:
  (i) distance to basket, (ii) fatigue, (iii) defender presence, (iv) visual information / quiet eye.
  Kinematic themes (youth):
  - Longer distance → often **lower release angle**, **higher release velocity**, greater shoulder/elbow
    angular velocities; more horizontal COM displacement in some cohorts.
  - Fatigue (~80% HRmax): **shooting percentage** drops for 2PT/3PT in some protocols; kinematic effects mixed.
  - Defender: **faster shot execution**, longer jump/flight times; contested gaze fixations shorter in some groups.
  - Practical implication repeated: train shooting under **game-like** constraints, not only static form.

3B. Narrative / systematic review — jump shot components (PAYWALLED)
  Okazaki, Rodacki & Satern, Sports Biomechanics, 2015.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2015.1052541
  Abstract structure (use for phase taxonomy seeds, verify against licensed full text if ingesting quotes):
  - Section A: variables affecting **ball trajectory** (release height, angle, velocity; entry angle themes).
  - Section B: **phases of the jump shot** (prep/load, jump, release — align to CeRebel setup/load/drive/release/follow).
  - Section C: additional modifiers (skill level, distance, fatigue, vision).
  License: Taylor & Francis — subscription; product uses paraphrased relationships only.

3C. Made vs missed jump shots — male recreational players (OPEN ACCESS, CC BY 4.0)
  Cabarkapa et al., Biomechanics (MDPI), 2022.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/biomechanics2030028
  PDF: https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/biomechanics/biomechanics-02-00028/article_deploy/biomechanics-02-00028.pdf
  Methods: force plate + 2D video; preparatory vs release phases.
  Findings (group level, not prescriptive for individuals):
  - 2PT makes: higher **elbow position** relative to stature in preparatory phase.
  - 3PT makes: more **erect torso** in prep, greater **release angle**, higher vertical jump at release,
    higher max trajectory height — alongside elbow placement themes.

3D. Youth U16/U18 2PT vs 3PT catch-and-shoot kinematics (PMC full text — check license block)
  Vencúrik et al., kinematic analysis European youth nationals (2017 cohort).
  PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7908352/
  Notes: 3D inertial suit + smart ball; catch-and-shoot after cut (game-like).
  - Males show larger vertical COM displacement on 2PT vs females; entry angle higher on successful 3PT attempts.
  - 3PT performed with higher “shooting speed” (ball contact time proxy) vs 2PT in most categories.
  Data not openly redistributed — cite published statistics only.

3E. Distance effects — elite youth (CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 — commercial: paraphrase only)
  Podmenik et al., J Hum Kinet, 2021.
  PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8120969/
  Longer distance → greater shoulder-axis rotation in transverse plane; landing deviation patterns change.

3F. Jump-shot “dip” and accuracy (OPEN ACCESS, CC BY — Frontiers)
  Penner, Front Psychol, 2021.
  PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8273237/
  Defines “dip” as lowering ball below shooting pocket before set point; experimental evidence that dip can
  **increase accuracy** vs no-dip in studied elite cohort — counters “dip is too slow” coaching dogma.
  Product maps dip to **load phase** knee/elbow flexion, not ball height (no ball track required).

3G. U18 skill level — 2PT jump shot vs free throw (OPEN ACCESS, CC BY 4.0)
  Comparative Analysis, MDPI / PMC 2024.
  PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11677033/
  Higher-level U18 males: faster release times and better entry angles on FT; higher 2PT success vs lower level.
  Emphasizes **entry angle** and release timing — product uses body proxies only unless ball track added later.

3H. Open-access jump-shot kinematics (Serbian journal PDF, CC BY-NC-ND)
  Fu Phys Ed Sport / University of Niš repository article on elite male jump shot release parameters.
  URL: https://casopisi.junis.ni.ac.rs/index.php/FUPhysEdSport/article/download/3908/3222
  Use: supplementary release-time / entry-angle context; respect NC-ND in commercial corpus.

4. CE REBEL PHASE / BODY-TRACKING IMPLICATIONS
================================================================================

Reconcile literature phases with pack phase IDs (brain_plan §3):
  setup → load (dip) → drive (jump) → release → follow → landing → recover

Detector anchors (body-only):
  - Release proxy: peak ||v(shooting_wrist)|| (not ball release).
  - Load: knee flexion minimum before drive.
  - Game context findings (defender, fatigue, distance) inform **Training/** constraints and disclaimers,
    not automatic fault thresholds from a single clip without context labels.

Youth vs adult separation (mandatory in product copy):
  - USA/FIBA youth materials: lower rim, closer FT line, no 3PT scoring bands — do not apply adult 3PT mechanics
    cues to ages 9–11.
  - Biomechanics numbers in reviews (release angles, °/s joint speeds) mostly from adolescent/elite lab cohorts —
    qualitative relationships only in Findings/ (leg drive before arm, variability across styles).

Inter-individual variability:
  - FIBA mini manual + Okazaki lineage: multiple successful shooting styles; avoid single “model shooter” angles.
  - One-motion vs two-motion, dip vs no-dip: style tags in taxonomy; faults flag **repeatability/timing**, not
    aesthetic match to one NBA form.
