1 Forword 
The FIS Technical Official General Rules is meant t o provide TO with guidance on some 
fundamental FIS rules applicable to their role in F IS. Content covers reimbursement rules, 
general Technical Official nomination rules, and re sponsibilities of Technical Delegates in 
case of sever accidents on the field of play. 
They are governed and managed by FIS. Requests for changes and/or adjustments can be 
made directly to FIS [events@fis-ski.com]. 
2 Technical Official Insurance 
2.1 Accident Insurance 
All Technical Officials must have their own accident insurance. If the Technical Official`s own 
insurance does not cover all the costs, FIS has a subsidiary cover in case of an accident for 
all Technical Officials working for FIS at an event.  
2.2 Health Insurance 
A general health insurance that covers forced leave in case of illness must be effected by the 
Technical Official. 
3 Reimbursement Rules for Technical Officials  
Costs of active engagement, i.e., FIS TO assignment s, are normally covered by the local 
organizing committee of the respective events. As per the rules, each TO receives a per diem 
reimbursement of 125 CHF (including travel days) an d accommodation with full board. 
Additionally, travel costs are covered up to a maximum of 1000 CHF (for OWG, PWG, WSC, 
JWSC, WC) and up to 600 CHF (all other events and e ducation, i.e. TD/Judge 
Seminars/Updates/Clinics) upon reception of the ori ginal receipts (Train 2nd class, Plane 
Economy class, Car mileage allowance of 0.70 CHF/km  including toll/vignette expenses)1. 
No other additional costs (i.e., phone, printing, etc) are covered either by FIS or the organizing 
committee. Expenses need to be claimed using the FIS Expense sheet outlined below.

5 General Technical Official Nomination Policy 
For governance reasons, the nomination and selection of any TO for a specific event 
should follow a transparent nomination policy that is rooted in 2: 
a) language skills, e.g.: English is a prerequisite  for all TO but if someone also knows the 
local language, they might be preferred for events at specific levels and/or specific 
regions. 
b) the state of education, e.g.: if someone is usin g all education opportunities including 
demonstrating being active as TO (national and/or international), they might be 
preferred for specific events.  
c) the quality of demonstrated work, including skil ls and personality, e.g.: if someone has 
a strong and positive record of work provided by different stakeholders, they might be 
preferred for specific events. 
d) the geographical location of residence and/or tr avel options, e.g.: the proximity of a TO 
to the event and/or the conditions of travel might be a criterion why they might be 
preferred for a specific event. 
e) the availability, e.g.: the availability of a TO  for a specific event might be a criterion why 
they might be preferred for a specific event

6 TD Guidelines in the Event of Serious Accidents  
One of the goals of FIS is to provide reliable data on injury trends in international skiing and 
snowboarding at the competition level. Specific objectives include:  
• Monitoring injury patterns in all FIS disciplines  
• Monitoring trends in injury risk with time  
• Providing potential background data for in-depth s tudies of the causes of injury for 
particular injury types in specific disciplines, e.g. knee and head injuries in alpine skiing, 
snowboarding and freestyle skiing.  
The overall goal of FIS is to support a reduction in injury rates which may include changes 
in rules and regulations, equipment or coaching techniques based on data provided. 
For this purpose, a reportable injury is defined as an injury that occurs during competition 
or official training and requires attention by medical personnel .  
 
For a solid evidence base, a detailed Injury Report is necessary. The TD plays an important 
role in registering (severe) incidents, but they ar e NOT  responsible in registering medical 
details. 
 
Roles and Responsibilities of the TD 
 
• Indicate in the TD report, that a (severe) inciden t happened during the event. Please 
specify: 
o The stakeholder group affected (i.e., athlete, off icial, LOC etc) 
o If an athlete and/or official, indicate the countr y/NSA 
o Contact details of either the NSA or LOC (LOC staf f) 
o If available, contact details from the medical sta ff treating the patient on site 
o The evacuation of the patient (walking, transport with a sledge, transport by 
helicopter) 
• Check whether tapes or videos were taken of the injury by team coaching staff or others 
and provide information on contact details to obtain a copy of the injury videotape. 
• Make notes about the incident as detailed as possi ble and think of anything that might 
be useful to reconstruct the incident (i.e., where did it happen, when did it happen, the 
weather conditions, who was involved etc) 
 
It is NOT  the duty of the TD to register any medical or othe r sensitive data nor to question 
anyone about these data. This will be done by the r espective departments within FIS in a 
second step with a validated Injury Registration Form.
