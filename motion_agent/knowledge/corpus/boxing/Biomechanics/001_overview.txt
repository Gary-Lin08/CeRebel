# Boxing Shadowboxing — Biomechanics Overview (open literature summaries)

Authority: primarily B+ (peer-reviewed striking kinematics and reviews) and B (coach-education synthesis).
This document supports RAG retrieval for **non-contact shadow form** — no impact forces, glove mass, or opponent
reaction. Not medical advice. See Docs/002_DISCLAIMER.txt.

================================================================================
1. WHY BIOMECHANICS MATTERS WITHOUT A TARGET
================================================================================

Shadowboxing removes impact but preserves the **coordination pattern** coaches care about: base of support, segment
sequencing from lower body to hand, and return to guard. Pose-only pipelines estimate joint angles and segment
speeds; they cannot measure punch force. Findings therefore describe **visible sequencing and posture**, not
Newtonian output at the glove.

Research on boxing and martial-arts straight punches generally reports contribution of **leg drive, hip/pelvis
rotation, and trunk rotation** to hand velocity and timing, with the arm acting as the final link in a chain
(B+). Shadow practice at submaximal speed still trains these relative timings when athletes avoid pure arm swinging.

================================================================================
2. KINETIC CHAIN (QUALITATIVE MODEL)
================================================================================

Common coaching and biomechanical descriptions for rear-hand straight punches (cross analog):

1. **Ground interaction**: Push from rear foot; rear heel may rise or pivot to permit hip rotation (style-dependent).
2. **Pelvis and trunk**: Rotation and slight weight transfer increase hand path velocity before peak elbow extension.
3. **Shoulder and elbow**: Elbow extends along a path constrained by guard discipline; excessive abduction changes
   shoulder loading patterns in lab studies (group-level, not individual prescription).
4. **Wrist and hand**: Peak linear speed of the hand/wrist proxy often occurs near maximum elbow extension in
   straight punches in controlled studies — product uses wrist speed peak as E_EXTENSION_PEAK.

Jabs (lead hand) may show **smaller hip rotation** but still often include weight shift or step in skilled athletes.
Flagging “arm-only” on all lead-hand strikes is intentionally conservative for rear-hand and hook/uppercut classes.

Hooks and uppercuts emphasize **angular momentum** and bent-elbow geometry; horizontal and vertical hooks show
different elbow flexion ranges in kinematic captures (B+). Shadow hooks should not appear as fully extended slaps if
the goal is fundamental skill transfer.

================================================================================
3. STANCE AND BASE OF SUPPORT
================================================================================

Boxing stance is a **split stance** with weight biased but not frozen on one leg. Normalized base width and knee
flexion affect:

- Lateral stability during slips and pivots.
- Time to initiate weight transfer toward the striking side.
- COM excursion during footwork — overstepping increases recovery time (coaching models, B).

Youth athletes often adopt a slightly wider base for balance; biomechanical comparisons between age groups are limited
— product uses qualitative youth bands in Quality docs rather than elite adult templates.

================================================================================
4. TRUNK ROTATION AND SPINE LOADING (CAREFUL WORDING)
================================================================================

Rotation for power punches comes from **hips and thorax**, not from extreme passive lumbar twisting alone. Coaching
cues that promote rear-foot pivot aim to distribute rotation through the kinetic chain (B).

Excessive end-range neck extension or flexion during slips and rolls is discouraged in safety-oriented coaching (B+)
because of habit formation for later contact training — **not** because shadowboxing alone causes concussion.

CeRebel does not diagnose spine or shoulder pathology from pose data.

================================================================================
5. RECOIL AND DECELERATION
================================================================================

After peak hand speed, **active retraction** (elbow flexion, shoulder retraction) is part of defensive habit.
Biomechanically, decelerating the hand uses shoulder and elbow musculature; slow or wide recoil paths are visible
in pose and correlate with coaching corrections but are not unique injury predictors in shadow-only contexts.

Rapid repeated maximal-speed punching without recovery may increase **local muscular fatigue** in shoulders — volume
guidance belongs in Training/001 and coach supervision, not automated diagnosis.

================================================================================
6. DEFENSIVE MOVEMENTS (HEAD AND TRUNK)
================================================================================

Slips: primarily **lateral translation and rotation of the head** relative to pelvis with knees flexed; hands remain
high in fundamental teaching (B).

Rolls: combine **trunk flexion and lateral offset**; knee flexion lowers COM and supports smooth return (B).

Pose estimation error is high for subtle head motion — slip/roll findings should run at lower default confidence.

10. YOUTH VS ADULT (BIOMECH)
================================================================================

Children and adolescents differ in limb length ratios, strength, and coordination maturity. Shadowboxing for youth
should emphasize **technique volume at controlled speed** rather than maximizing wrist speed peaks relative to adult
norms from literature (often college or elite samples).

Separate youth checkpoints in Quality/002; do not merge adult kinematic ranges into youth pass/fail without an age tag.

================================================================================
11. JAB (LEAD STRAIGHT) — SHADOW BIOMECHANICS
================================================================================

The jab is the primary **lead-hand straight** in orthodox shadowboxing. Biomechanically it is often a
shorter impulse than the rear straight: less trunk rotation in many skilled athletes, but still rarely a
pure arm swing when coached for fundamentals (B+).

Body-visible priorities for pose-only coaching (no opponent, no impact):

- **Rear-hand integrity**: Rear wrist should remain in the guard height band while the lead hand extends.
  Dropping the rear hand is a defensive habit error visible in lab and field coaching, not a “power” cue.
- **Extension path**: Lead elbow extends along a forward axis with moderate abduction; wide elbow flare
  changes shoulder moment arm and is flagged in kinematic studies as style- or skill-dependent variance.
- **Extension proxy**: Product timing uses argmax ||v(lead_wrist)|| in the punch window — this is peak
  hand speed in space, not contact force or intent to harm.
- **Retraction**: Active elbow flexion and shoulder retraction return the lead hand to guard before the
  next prep peak; incomplete retraction shows as sustained lead-wrist speed (rhythm fault in Quality/002).

Youth shadow jabs: prioritize **control and return** over maximizing lead-wrist speed relative to adult
literature samples (often trained or collegiate athletes).

================================================================================
12. CROSS (REAR STRAIGHT) — SHADOW BIOMECHANICS
================================================================================

The cross (rear-hand straight in orthodox) typically shows **greater pelvis and trunk rotation** than the
jab in group kinematic comparisons (B+). Coaching heuristics that match visible body signals:

- **Foot and hip**: Rear-foot push and trail-foot pivot on the ball permit hip yaw toward the lead side;
  a flat rear foot during rotation is a common teaching correction (heuristic, not a universal degree target).
- **Sequencing**: Hip/trunk rotation often rises before or coincident with rear-wrist speed peak; an
  arm-only cross shows rear-wrist peak with minimal hip-rotation rise (fault_arm_only_punch in Findings/001).
- **Weight transfer**: COM shifts toward the lead leg through extension in many athletes; excessive lateral
  sway outside the foot polygon is a balance finding, not a combat tactic.
- **Retraction**: Rear hand must return to guard band after extension — lingering forward hand speed is
  visible in shadow and correlates with open guard in later training contexts (form only).

Southpaw athletes mirror lead/rear joint naming in product logic; biomechanical descriptions above swap
hand labels, not the sequencing principles.

================================================================================
14. NON-HARM AND PRODUCT BOUNDARY (REINFORCEMENT)
================================================================================

This pack describes **movement quality for fitness and skill development** in non-contact shadowboxing.
It does not instruct how to strike a person, maximize injury to an opponent, or select targets on the body.
Biomechanics of head impact and effective mass at glove contact appear in research literature only to
support why federations teach compact guard and stable neck posture — not to optimize harm.

Stop activity on pain; seek licensed care for injury or concussion concerns (Docs/002_DISCLAIMER.txt).
