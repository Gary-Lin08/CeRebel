# General Human Motion Biomechanics — Overview (evidence-aware summary)

This document summarizes **open-access and widely cited research themes** for CeRebel body-first general coaching.
It does not prescribe individual medical treatment or guarantee injury prevention. Numeric values are
**cohort ranges or qualitative heuristics** — not universal targets for every person.

Authority key: A+ public health / WHO | A peer-reviewed clinical | B+ strong reviews |
B coaching consensus | ProductLogic internal measurement definitions.

Corpus pack id: **human**. Product pack id: **general**. Measurement policy sibling:
`motion_agent/knowledge/corpus/opensim_motion_tools.txt`.

---

## 1. Posture stack and segment coordination (B+)

Human movement quality in coaching contexts often references an **axial stack**: head balanced over thorax,
ribs organized over pelvis, pelvis over base of support. Biomechanically this is not rigid “straight spine”
but **controlled alignment** that limits unnecessary shear and compensatory motion when transferring force
between lower and upper body.

Trunk–pelvis **coordination** (phase locking, counter-rotation) appears across walking, running, throwing,
and striking. Literature in *Gait & Posture* and related journals describes coupling between pelvis rotation
and thorax rotation with activity-dependent phase lags — authority **B+**. CeRebel general pack uses coarse
phases (prep → action → recover) rather than activity-specific coupling angles.

**Product implication**: stack findings (`posture_stack_break`, `forward_head`) describe **visible organization**,
not clinical postural syndrome labels.

---

## 2. Forward head and upper quarter kinematics (B+)

Forward head posture (FHP) in sagittal plane is common in sedentary populations. Studies associate increased
**neck extensor moment** and altered scapular mechanics with sustained forward head positions in laboratory
setups — authority **B+** (multiple EMG/kinematic studies; effect sizes vary). Video pose estimation provides
**proxy angles only**; cervical diagnosis requires clinical exam.

Youth: growing athletes may show different neck–trunk proportions; avoid adult FHP thresholds on U12 without
widened bands (developmental themes, authority B+).

---

## 3. Pelvis orientation and lumbopelvic rhythm (B+)

**Anterior pelvic tilt** (APT) and **posterior pelvic tilt** describe pelvis orientation relative to femurs and
trunk. APT alone is not disease; it interacts with lumbar curvature and hip flexion range during squat and
hinge tasks. Lumbopelvic rhythm during forward bend ideally shares flexion between **hip and lumbar spine**;
excessive lumbar share is coached as “spine-dominant” pattern — authority **B+** in movement screening
literature (summarized, not Sahrmann syndrome diagnosis in product).

OpenSim pipelines may report pelvis tilt angles in radians or degrees — cite as **measurement** when tools run;
otherwise 2D proxy with lower confidence per opensim_motion_tools.txt policy.

---

## 4. Postural control, COM, and balance (B+)

**Center of mass (COM)** trajectory relative to **base of support (BOS)** underpins balance. Quiet standing
shows small anteroposterior and mediolateral sway; single-leg tasks increase sway path length. Reviews on
postural control in *Sports Medicine* and PMC corpora summarize age-related sway increases — authority **B+**.

CeRebel uses pelvis or model COM **lateral sway** proxies for `single_leg_balance_wobble`. This is not a
clinical balance test (e.g., BESS scoring replacement).

---

## 5. Frontal-plane knee alignment (B+)

**Dynamic knee valgus** (knee medial to hip–ankle line during flexion) is studied heavily in jump-landing and
squat tasks regarding **knee loading distribution**. Neuromuscular training programs can reduce valgus in
some cohorts — authority **B+** (ACL prevention meta-analyses themes). Straight-line walking and generic
clips show smaller valgus excursions; product gates finding on frontal/oblique capture confidence.

**Youth**: valgus may appear during growth and skill acquisition; coach alignment without fear language.

---

## 6. Hip hinge vs lumbar flexion (B+)

Hip-dominant flexion (**hip hinge**) shifts moment demand toward hip extensors; lumbar-flexion-dominant
patterns concentrate flexion in spine segments. NSCA and peer-reviewed lifting biomechanics describe hinge
as fundamental for picks and swings — authority **B/B+**. Individual hamstring flexibility and anthropometry
alter visible shape; no single “correct” back angle for all.

Finding `stiff_spine_no_hip_hinge` uses **pattern heuristics** (hip flexion share vs lumbar) not diagnosis of
flexion intolerance.

---

## 7. Bilateral asymmetry (B+)

Left–right differences in shoulder height, limb ROM, and force production are **normal within bands**.
Athletes show limb dominance; asymmetry beyond habitual band may reflect guarding or prior injury — association
with injury is inconsistent in literature — authority **B+** for variability themes, **not** diagnostic from video.

Product compares **within-clip** left vs right ROM at action peak for symmetric tasks only.

---

## 8. Movement phases and timing (B)

Motor learning literature emphasizes **preparation**, **execution**, and **feedback** phases. CeRebel maps these
to setup/prep/load/action/follow_through/recover/reset for sport-agnostic coaching. Abrupt starts (`rushed_prep`)
reduce time to organize stack — coaching consensus **B**, supported by skill acquisition themes.

---

## 9. OpenSim and monocular inverse dynamics (B, method)

OpenSim musculoskeletal models estimate joint angles, moments, and muscle activations from kinematics with
assumed dynamics. **Ground reaction forces** from monocular pipelines are **estimated**, not force-plate truth
(opensim_motion_tools.txt). General pack coaching should not cite peak knee moment numbers from low-confidence
ID unless uncertainty is disclosed.

Authority **B** for method; coaching claims remain B/B+ from human movement literature.

## 11. What this pack does NOT claim

- No diagnosis of scoliosis, leg length discrepancy, or nerve compression from pose.
- No optimal “neutral spine” angle as medical prescription.
- No replacement for clinical balance or concussion assessment.
- No sport-specific technique optima (defer to sport packs).
