# General Human Motion — Quality Definition (body-first)

## What “good movement quality” means in CeRebel (general pack)

Quality is **not** a single score in v1. The product describes how well visible body organization aligns with
checkpoints derived from postural control literature and fundamental coaching — without claiming one ideal pose
for every body, sport, or task.

Quality is evaluated in five dimensions. Each dimension links to checkpoints in `002_checkpoints.txt` and may
trigger finding IDs when failed.

### Dimension 1 — Axial stack and head–pelvis alignment

**Intent**: Head, ribs, and pelvis stay organized in a coherent stack during setup and under load; excessive
forward head or rib flare does not dominate the pattern.

Body signals:
- Stack alignment proxy (head vs shoulder midline vs pelvis in sagittal/frontal composition)
- Forward head angle proxy when sagittal view available
- Rib cage angle vs pelvis in load phases

Failed checkpoints may trigger: `posture_stack_break`, `forward_head`, `ribcage_flare_anterior`,
`pelvis_anterior_tilt_excess`.

Evidence band: B+ for trunk–pelvis coordination themes; B for coaching cues.

### Dimension 2 — Balance and weight transfer

**Intent**: Center of mass moves deliberately over the base of support during prep and single-leg moments;
recovery returns to stable standing without large sway.

Body signals:
- Lateral COM sway magnitude during single-leg load
- Timing of weight shift vs action peak (prep duration ratio)
- Excessive whole-body lean to one side

Failed checkpoints may trigger: `single_leg_balance_wobble`, `insufficient_weight_shift`,
`excessive_lateral_lean`.

Evidence band: B+ postural control literature; B for drill selection.

### Dimension 3 — Lower-limb alignment and hip hinge

**Intent**: When bending or accepting load, hips contribute flexion; knees track without extreme inward collapse
on visible camera plane.

Body signals:
- Hip vs lumbar flexion share during prep/load (pattern heuristic)
- Frontal-plane knee angle proxy at max flexion
- Ankle–knee–hip alignment in frontal view

Failed checkpoints may trigger: `stiff_spine_no_hip_hinge`, `knee_valgus_visible`.

Evidence band: B+ neuromuscular training and squat/lunge kinematics themes (non-diagnostic).

### Dimension 4 — Limb–torso coordination and timing

**Intent**: Arms and legs move in sync with trunk; preparation is long enough to organize stack before peak speed.

Body signals:
- Thorax yaw vs pelvis yaw mismatch during prep/action
- Prep phase duration / action peak timing ratio
- Arm path crossing body chaotically vs task context (generic flag)

Failed checkpoints may trigger: `arms_fight_torso`, `rushed_prep`.

Evidence band: B+ segment coupling reviews; B coaching.

### Dimension 5 — Symmetry and repeatable recovery

**Intent**: Left and right sides show comparable shoulder level and limb ROM when task is bilateral; athlete
completes recover phase toward neutral between reps.

Body signals:
- Shoulder height difference at setup
- Peak wrist/ankle ROM left vs right in action phase
- Recover phase stack variance vs setup baseline

Failed checkpoints may trigger: `shoulder_height_asymmetry`, `limb_rom_asymmetry`, `incomplete_recover`.

Evidence band: B for symmetry coaching; B+ for variability themes (not injury diagnosis).

## Youth vs adult quality interpretation

Youth:
- Stack varies with growth; mild shoulder height difference may appear — widen asymmetry bands U14.
- Do not apply adult static “perfect posture” language; emphasize control and pain-free range.

Adult:
- Apply adult checkpoint bands in 002; desk-work forward head common — coach without shaming.

Masters:
- Slower prep and longer recover may be normal; product may tag `audience=masters` in future without changing phase IDs.

## Relationship to findings severity

Checkpoints produce **coaching priorities**, not medical grades. A failed knee-valgus proxy suggests alignment
awareness and strength work themes — it does not diagnose ACL risk or pathology.

## Repeatability and capture quality

Quality analysis confidence drops when:
- FPS < 24, heavy blur, or occluded pelvis/knees
- Camera not approximately frontal for knee/shoulder symmetry claims
- Clip shorter than one full prep–action–recover cycle
- OpenSim tools unavailable and only low-confidence 2D pose exists

Document low-confidence state in coach briefs; KB defines concepts only. When tools are available, prefer
measured series per `opensim_motion_tools.txt`.
