# Yoga — Group Class, Studio, and Team Practice (capture + coaching workflow)

Body-first alignment sessions for studios, school programs, athletic teams using yoga as cross-training,
and corporate wellness groups. Authority: B coaching consensus; safety themes align with NCCIH consumer
summaries (A) cited in `_meta/SOURCE_MANIFEST.txt`. This is **not** medical screening or load prescription.

See Docs/002_DISCLAIMER.txt: modifications are coaching options, not diagnosis or eligibility tests.

---

## 1. Why film holds in a group setting

Group yoga differs from solo home practice:

- **Teacher attention is split** — short clips give each practitioner one or two actionable cues per week.
- **Flow classes** mix entry, hold, and transition; CeRebel v1 prioritizes **steady hold windows** (Y_ALIGN, Y_HOLD).
- **Line-of-sight** varies by mat placement; standardize camera angle per room when possible.

Session goal: one **repeatable capture setup**, one **pose family** per filming block, and **homework**
(one drill from Coaching/002 per person) rather than correcting every fault in one class.

---

## 2. Pre-class movement prep (before recording)

Typical 8–12 minute warm-up before filming alignment holds (B studio practice patterns):

1. **Breath and neutral stand** (1–2 min): Mountain pose — establish baseline; do not film until stillness.
2. **Joint mobility**: wrist circles, shoulder rolls, cat–cow at tabletop pace (not max range contests).
3. **Progressive load**: puppy or wall dog → plank on knees → full plank only if line holds.
4. **Balance prep**: tree at wall or chair fingertip touch — film only when practitioner opts in without forcing depth.

Youth / school groups: shorten to 5–8 minutes; emphasize playful balance and **no pain** language.
Prenatal or postpartum participants: **do not** use automated findings as clearance; refer to qualified prenatal teacher.

Avoid filming the first minute after cold entry from outdoors — stiffness skews fold and dog shapes.

---

## 3. Camera setup for studios and gyms

**Downward dog, plank, chaturanga (sagittal preferred)**:
- Camera at **hip height**, **perpendicular** to long edge of mat, 2.5–4 m away.
- Full body in frame including hands and feet; avoid cropping heels in dog.
- Practitioner sideways to camera (profile) for lumbar and hip sag visibility.

**Warrior I / II, triangle, chair (45° or frontal)**:
- **45° front-diagonal** improves knee–ankle tracking vs pure frontal foreshortening.
- For warrior I pelvis “squareness,” add a **second short clip** from side if product supports multi-clip compare.

**Tree and single-leg balance**:
- Frontal camera centered on pelvis; wall support visible in frame is acceptable (note in session log).

**Cobra / low bridge (prone)**:
- Low camera at mat level, side view; head neutral — neck extension findings downgrade when face turns away.

**Room constraints**:
- Minimize mirrors doubling skeleton noise if pose backend allows mirror masking; else angle away from mirrors.
- Even lighting on mat; backlit windows reduce edge confidence on wrists and ankles.

Multiple practitioners: **one mat lane at a time** for v1 analysis quality; queue others in parallel breath work.

## 4. Class formats mapped to capture strategy

| Format | Capture approach | Phase emphasis |
|--------|------------------|----------------|
| Slow flow / alignment | Pause 3–5 breaths in target pose | Y_ALIGN, Y_HOLD |
| Vinyasa | Isolate **one** pose per week for homework film | Y_HOLD only in clip |
| Yin / restorative | Long holds — good for steadiness (com_drift) | Y_HOLD |
| Power / heated | Film early block before fatigue sag dominates | Y_ALIGN early |
| Team cross-training | Plank, low lunge, dog as mobility screen | F_TABLETOP_CORE, F_INVERTED_V |

Athletic teams: treat yoga as **supplemental** mobility — one finding per athlete per microcycle;
coordinate with AT/PT staff; never override medical hold orders with app cues.

## 5. Coach / teacher workflow with phase scrub

Target UX for studio owners using CeRebel review:

1. Tag clip: **pose_id**, **experience band** (youth | adult), **prop use** (wall, block, chair).
2. Review clip-level **hold steadiness** and top severity finding (form vs load hint).
3. Scrub to **Y_ALIGN** window (see Phases/001) for stacking faults (knee, lumbar, shoulders).
4. Assign **one cue** (Coaching/001) + **one 30–90 s drill** (Coaching/002) including modification if linked.
5. Re-film same pose after 2–3 weeks at same camera geometry — qualitative compare, not competition on depth.

Language in group feedback: use **feel** and **option** framing (“many practitioners bend knees here”),
not diagnostic labels (“your disc is at risk”).

## 7. Props and modifications in team context

Props are **optional coaching tools**, not requirements for detection:

- **Wall**: dog, half-forward fold, tree — reduces fear and improves capture repeatability.
- **Chair**: uttanasana, warrior II hands — youth and tight hamstrings.
- **Blocks**: under hands in dog/plank — wrist angle changes; note prop in clip metadata.
- **Knees down**: plank/chaturanga progression — valid modification, not “failed” rep.

Document prop use so findings that assume mat-level hands are interpreted with lower confidence.

Caution entities (brain_plan §6): if participant reports acute wrist or low-back sensitivity, skip automated
“deeper” cues; offer modification_ids from Coaching/002 and suggest qualified in-person teacher review.

---

## 8. Weekly structure (illustrative — not prescriptive load)

This pack does **not** set minutes on mat or intensity for medical populations.

General B-pattern for alignment-focused groups:

- **Week A**: Inverted V family film block (dog) + homework pedaling dog.
- **Week B**: Tabletop / plank family + knee plank drill.
- **Week C**: Standing lunge family (warrior I/II) + short stance cue.
- **Week D**: Fold + gentle extension (uttanasana, cobra) + micro-bend emphasis.

Youth classes: rotate games that embed balance and plank **without** max-depth contests.
Corporate wellness: 20-minute capture-friendly subset (mountain, chair, dog at wall, tree at wall).

## 9. Privacy, consent, and inclusion

- Obtain **photo/video consent** per studio policy before filming for analysis.
- Avoid single-athlete public shaming; share findings privately or via coach dashboard.
- Body diversity: alignment heuristics vary; widen interpretation bands and prioritize comfort language.
- Gender-neutral cueing; avoid assuming hip structure from pose shape alone.

## 10. Integration with other CeRebel packs

Teams that also run **running** or **weightlifting** may share themes (hip hinge, shoulder depression).
Cross-link at coach discretion — yoga pack cautions against aggressive end-range pushing for lifters with
fatigue. No automatic merging of findings across sports in v1.

---

## 12. Limits of group practice analysis

- Cannot detect breath retention, dizziness, or pain — participants must self-report and stop.
- Cannot replace hands-on assists or therapeutic assessment.
- Multi-person frames: analyze one skeleton only per clip in v1.
