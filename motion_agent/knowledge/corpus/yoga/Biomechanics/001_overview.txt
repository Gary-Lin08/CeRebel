# Yoga Biomechanics — Overview (open literature summaries)
# Authority: B+ primary; B for coaching crossover | Not prescriptive medicine
# Product use: qualitative relationships for RAG; no universal degree norms

================================================================================
1. SCOPE AND METHOD
================================================================================

This document summarizes **peer-reviewed and widely cited open-access reviews** on yoga-related
musculoskeletal mechanics, plus established exercise-science relationships applied to common mat poses.
CeRebel uses these relationships to **explain why coaching cues exist**, not to diagnose injury or assign
“correct” angles to every body.

Key methodological limits for video pose estimation:
- 2D projection error dominates sagittal “stacking” and knee–toe alignment.
- Surface markers approximate joint centers; yoga alignment is often taught in 3D while camera is 2D.
- Passive vs active range cannot be distinguished from shape alone.

================================================================================
2. SPINE — FLEXION, EXTENSION, AXIAL LOAD
================================================================================

2.1 Standing and seated forward folds (Uttanasana, Paschimottanasana)

Biomechanical theme: **hip flexion** should contribute substantially to forward bending; **lumbar flexion**
increases compressive and shear loads on vertebral segments in cadaveric and modeling studies of flexion
(general spine biomechanics, not yoga-specific trials alone).

Yoga therapy and LBP systematic reviews (Cramer et al., multiple Cochrane-adjacent summaries, B+) report
**benefit for some chronic low back pain cohorts** with supervised, modified yoga — individual response varies;
pain during movement is typically a stop signal in clinical yoga protocols.

Product mapping:
- fault_forward_fold_spine_round — visible lumbar flexion dominance before hip hinge.
- Checkpoints emphasize hip hinge and props; osteoporosis disclaimer overrides depth messaging.

2.2 Downward-Facing Dog — mixed chain

Down Dog is **not a pure forward fold** nor a handstand. Kinematically it combines:
- Shoulder flexion often 130–180° depending on mobility (literature ranges vary by measurement method).
- Hip flexion with posterior weight shift; knee flexion modulates hamstring tension.
- Wrist extension load in closed kinetic chain.

Shoulder girdle: serratus anterior and lower trapezius contribute to upward rotation and scapular stability
in weight-bearing shoulder flexion (push-up/plank EMG literature, B+). **Shrugging** (upper trap dominance)
is a common compensation when hamstrings or shoulders are limited — maps to fault_dog_shoulder_elevation.

Lumbar: instructors often cue **anteverted pelvis with long spine** vs **sagging** between shoulder and hip
hyperextension — product uses qualitative sagittal line, not single pelvic tilt degree.

2.3 Backbends — Cobra, Upward Dog

Extension distributes across thoracic and lumbar segments; **lumbar-dominant extension** shows larger local
angles when thoracic region is stiff (clinical observation + spine extension studies, B+).

Up Dog adds **lower extremity load** (knees lifted, tops of feet) and **wrist extension** with body mass
over hands — shoulder position relative to wrist alters joint moments similar to plank family.

Cervical extension additive to thoracic extension increases **regional cervical load** — coaching neutral neck
aligns with conservative PT yoga modifications (B).

2.4 Twists (supine twist in common set)

Spinal rotation couples with subtle lateral flexion; **forced end-range twist** under load is discouraged
for osteoporosis and acute disc presentations in clinical yoga guidelines (B+). Product v1 includes gentle
supine twist in taxonomy with checkpoint on shoulder grounding — not aggressive bind poses.

================================================================================
3. LOWER EXTREMITY — KNEE, HIP, ANKLE
================================================================================

3.1 Lunges and Warrior family

Front knee flexion in lunge/Warrior II increases **patellofemoral joint contact stress** with flexion angle
in biomechanical models (squat/lunge literature, B+). Hence cues for **knee over ankle** and **tracking over
toes** — not because a single angle is universal, but because extreme forward knee translation with deep flexion
raises modeled PFJ load.

Frontal plane: **dynamic knee valgus** (knee medial to hip–foot line) is associated with increased valgus
moment in drop-jump studies; yoga applies analogous visual cue in Warrior — fault_warrior_knee_collapse.

Hip **external rotation and abduction** demands differ Warrior II (open pelvis) vs Warrior I (square cue).
Skeletal **femoral anteversion/version** limits apparent “square hips” — anatomical variation (B+) must be
acknowledged to avoid harmful shame-based alignment (fault_w1_hip_open low severity).

3.2 Chair Pose (Utkatasana)

Resembles partial squat with torso inclined; quadriceps eccentric/concentric load high. Knee valgus same
concern as squat — fault_chair_knee_collapse.

3.3 Tree and single-leg stance

Pelvic **Trendelenburg** (contralateral hip drop) indicates hip abductor insufficiency or fatigue in clinical
gait analysis; in Tree appears as fault_balance_hip_drop / fault_tree_hip_jut.

Knee **hyperextension** in standing increases passive ligament stress in recurvatum populations — micro-bend
cue (fault_locked_knee_balance).

3.4 Down Dog heels and ankle

Ankle dorsiflexion limits affect heel height; **forcing heels down** is not a biomechanical requirement for
spine length — primarily gastrocnemius/soleus extensibility. Product does not fail short dorsiflexion.

================================================================================
4. UPPER EXTREMITY — SHOULDER, ELBOW, WRIST
================================================================================

4.1 Plank and Chaturanga

Plank maintains **scapular protraction/retraction balance**; sagging hips often coincide with scapular winging
or lumbar extension (fault_plank_hip_sag).

Chaturanga is a **closed-chain push-up** with high elbow and shoulder demand. Biomechanical push-up studies show:
- Elbow flare increases shoulder abduction angle and may alter subacromial space dynamics in some models (debated, B+).
- **Shoulders below elbows** (fault_chaturanga_shoulder_drop) is discouraged in yoga transition safety articles
  citing shoulder impingement risk in repetitive vinyasa (mechanism plausible, individual risk varies).

Eccentric control reduces peak rate of loading — fault_flow_uncontrolled_drop.

4.2 Wrist extension in weight-bearing poses

Extended wrist angles in Dog, Plank, Up Dog increase **wrist extension moment**. Modifications (fists, blocks,
forearm dog) redistribute load — coaching props, not tracked.

4.3 Overhead reach (Upward Salute, Chair arms)

Shoulder flexion with **rib cage extension** (fault_upward_salute_rib_flare) reflects limited thoracic extension
or habitual lumbar compensation in overhead patterns seen in strength & conditioning literature (B).

================================================================================
5. CORE AND INTRATHORACIC PRESSURE (QUALITATIVE)
================================================================================

“Core engagement” in plank/chair reduces lumbar shear in some trunk modeling (B+). Product does not measure IAP
or breath holding; **breath-holding during effort** is discouraged in general exercise safety (B).

Ujjayi and other pranayama are **out of scope** for pose-only pipeline.

================================================================================
6. LOAD, FREQUENCY, AND ADAPTATION
================================================================================

Yoga practice spans restorative (low load) to power/vinyasa (moderate–high repetitive wrist/shoulder/knee load).
Systematic reviews note **dose-response** is study-dependent; 1–3 sessions/week supervised often appear in trials
for LBP and balance in older adults (B+).

Adaptation targets:
- **Neuromuscular**: balance, proprioception (Tree, Warrior III prep).
- **Mobility**: active ROM with control, not passive end-range forcing.
- **Strength**: isometric holds (Plank, Chair, Warrior), eccentric chaturanga.

Youth: growth plates and apophyseal sites — high repetitive end-range jump-back vinyasa less emphasized in
youth training content; prefer short holds and games.

Older adults: fall prevention evidence for balance components of yoga/tai chi class comparisons (B+); chair
support and wall use increase safety.

================================================================================
7. HYPERMOBILITY
================================================================================

Generalized joint hypermobility (including hEDS spectrum) predisposes to **passive range without muscular control**.
Yoga culture “deeper is better” conflicts with PT guidance favoring **mid-range stability** (A/B+ clinical consensus
summaries). Product findings that reward “more angle” are avoided; drift and end-range collapse prioritized.

10. WHAT WE DO NOT CLAIM
================================================================================

- Specific joint angles as medical norms for all bodies.
- That correcting a visible fault reduces injury probability in an individual.
- That yoga replaces strength training or PT for pathological conditions.
- Breath, mental health, or spiritual outcomes from pose shape alone.

See Docs/002_DISCLAIMER.txt for product-safe language.
