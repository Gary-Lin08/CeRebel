# Yoga — Quality Definition (body-first)

## What “quality” means in CeRebel

For this pack, **pose quality** is not aesthetic perfection or “deepest stretch.” It is the degree to which visible
body mechanics align with patterns associated in open literature and reputable teaching with:

1. **Sustainable stacking** — load-bearing joints aligned so muscular support, not passive ligament stress, carries
   the pose (within capture limits).
2. **Stable hold** — after entry, center-of-mass and key segment angles remain controlled with breath-scale motion only.
3. **Appropriate range** — movement stays within active range the practitioner can control without compensatory
   collapse at adjacent joints (spine, knees, shoulders).
4. **Symmetric effort** — left–right differences within expected bounds for asymmetric poses (Warrior) vs unintended
   collapse (Tree hip drop, Plank shoulder shrug asymmetry).
5. **Controlled transitions** — in flow mode, peak-load joints (elbows, lumbar spine, front knee) show deceleration
   into arrival rather than uncontrolled drop.

Quality scores in product code (future) combine checkpoint pass rate, pose_id confidence, phase confidence, and capture
quality flags — not a single “Instagram shape” template.

Each dimension maps to checkpoints in `002_checkpoints.txt`. A hold can be “high core_line, moderate balance_stability”
— product copy should show dimension breakdown, not one grade.

## Evidence basis

- Systematic reviews on yoga for musculoskeletal health and low back pain (B+): emphasis on individualization,
  modified poses, and avoiding pain-provoking end range.
- Biomechanics of plank, push-up, and lunge kinematics (B+): scapular and trunk role, patellofemoral load with knee
  flexion angle — qualitative use only in product.
- Teaching lineage consensus (B): micro-bend at knee, hip hinge, “length before depth,” props — translated to body signals.
- Hypermobility clinical guidance (A/B+): stability and control over passive range — referenced for severity on
  end-range faults, not diagnosis.

## Youth vs adult quality framing

**Youth (approx. ≤14):** prioritize balance play, shorter holds (15–30 s), and fun stability over deep forward fold
or aggressive backbend shape. Larger sway in Tree is normal; coach foot placement away from knee.

**Adult:** same dimensions; allow greater ROM when checkpoints pass and no drift into fault patterns.

**Older adult / low bone density tag (user-declared):** downgrade depth checkpoints for forward fold and twist;
 emphasize spinal neutral and prop use in coaching copy — automated pack reads disclaimer flag if product adds it.

Never tell users to force heels down in Down Dog or full split in Triangle as universal quality markers.

## Capture quality gates

Downgrade or suppress findings when:

- Camera purely overhead or behind practitioner for sagittal-line poses (Plank, Dog).
- Legs occluded in seated forward fold.
- Baggy clothing hides knee alignment.
- Frame rate < 24 fps on flow transitions (misses E_PEAK_LOAD).
- User `pose_id` mismatch vs classifier (confidence < threshold).

## Relationship to findings

Checkpoints pass/fail/unclear at phase windows. Findings fire when checkpoints fail with confidence or fault-specific
rules in Findings/001 trigger. Quality definition states the positive model; Findings name common deviations.
