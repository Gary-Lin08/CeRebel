================================================================================
0. SCOPE & EVIDENCE USE
================================================================================

- Findings must be measurable on the body: pelvis/torso rotation, shoulder/elbow/wrist
  angles, knee flexion, split step, lunge depth, head stability, segment timing.
- Do NOT productize: ball speed, spin, landing in/out, toss placement, net clearance,
  foot-fault legality (requires court lines), racket string tension.
- Pooled kinematic degrees from meta-analyses are research aggregates across mixed
  methods and skill levels—not clinical “correct angles” for users.
- Separate youth (Red/Orange/Green, shorter rackets) from adult full-court mechanics.

1. FEDERATION RULES (BODY-RELEVANT EXCERPTS ONLY)
================================================================================

Authority A+ | License: © ITF Limited (2026 Rules PDF)
URL: https://www.itftennis.com/media/7221/2026-rules-of-tennis-english.pdf
Hub: https://www.itftennis.com/en/about-us/governance/rules-and-regulations/

Facts useful for phase labeling (not automated officiating):
- Rule 16–17: Service begins when server releases ball (hand) then strikes it before
  it hits the ground; server alternates deuce/ad sides each game.
- Rule 18 Foot fault: during service motion, server shall not touch baseline or court
  with foot; shall not touch area outside sideline with foot. Body-first cue: load leg
  and drive leg stay behind/toe line until contact (visible in side/rear camera).
- Rule 29 Continuous play: pace of play between points (training context only).
- Appendix VIII (10-and-under): mandated slower balls and shorter courts for sanctioned
  10U competition; yellow ball not permitted in those events.

USTA US play context A+ | License: © USTA (Friend at Court 2024)
URL: https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2024-pdfs/friend-at-court.pdf
- Contains ITF Rules of Tennis plus USTA regulations and “The Code” (player conduct).
- Product: cite ITF for global stroke rules; USTA for US organizational context only.

2. YOUTH SCALING (MOVEMENT ENVIRONMENT, NOT ADULT SERVE PEAKS)
================================================================================

Authority B | ITF Play and Stay / Tennis10s
URL: https://www.itftennis.com/en/growing-the-game/itf-tennis-play-and-stay/

- Red (Stage 3): ~75% slower ball; court length 10.97–12.80 m, width 4.27–6.10 m;
  net 0.800–0.838 m; ages up to ~8; rackets up to ~58 cm.
- Orange (Stage 2): ~50% slower; court 17.68–18.29 m × 6.10–8.23 m; net up to 0.914 m;
  ages ~8–10.
- Green (Stage 1): ~25% slower; full-size court; advanced 9–10; longer rackets.
- Body-first implication: same joint categories (split, rotate, swing) but smaller
  amplitude, lower absolute speeds; do not apply adult serve kinetic-chain peak timing
  to Red/Orange without youth-specific sources.

3. SERVE — PHASE MODEL & KINETIC CHAIN (BODY)
================================================================================

Authority A (clinical framework) | Kovacs & Ellenbecker 2011, Sports Health (PMC)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3445225/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/1941738111414175

Three phases, eight stages (visible body events for detectors):
  Preparation: (1) start, (2) release (ball hand), (3) loading, (4) cocking
  Acceleration: (5) acceleration, (6) contact
  Follow-through: (7) deceleration, (8) finish

Body-first facts:
- Effective serve uses synchronous lower-body (quads, hamstrings, hip rotators) then
  trunk then upper limb—whole-chain evaluation matters, not isolated arm cues.
- Loading stage: leg drive correlates with service velocity; common foot positions
  “foot-up” vs “foot-back” during loading (both used at elite level).
- Acceleration: rear shoulder and pelvis tilt store energy; rapid reversal of lumbar
  rotation accompanies racket acceleration before impact.
- Inverse relationship between spin and speed at elite level (do not infer spin from
  body pose alone without ball data).

Authority B+ | Jacquier-Bret et al. 2024 meta-analysis (PMC, CC BY 4.0)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11260724/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2024.1432030

Key points of interest (KPOI) for 3D/video body analysis:
- Trophy position: among most-studied—trunk inclination ~25.0° ± 7.1 (pooled);
  front-knee flexion ~64.5° ± 9.7 (pooled). High between-study method variance.
- Racket low point: shoulder lateral rotation ~130.1° ± 26.5 commonly reported.
- Ball impact: shoulder elevation ~110.7° ± 16.9; elbow flexion ~30.1° ± 15.9 (pooled).
- Many joints not consistently measured across studies—product should prefer relative
  timing and symmetry over absolute angle targets.

Authority B+ | Lambrich & Muehlbauer 2023 scoping review (PMC, CC BY 4.0)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10434869/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0290320

- 23 studies (229 players, ages 18–62): kinetic, kinematic, EMG comparisons of serve
  and groundstroke types/directions/stances show inconsistent significant effects.
- Heterogeneity drivers: age, sex, skill (rec/intermediate/advanced), force plate vs
  insoles, mocap vs video vs IMU. Product findings should tag skill band or avoid numbers.
- Research gaps: limited EMG for serve stance; limited kinetic work on groundstroke types.

4. FOREHAND & GROUNDSTROKES (BODY)
================================================================================

Authority B+ | Reid, Elliott, Crespo 2013 forehand review (PMC, JSSM)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3761830/
Also: https://jssm.org/vol12/n2/2/v12n2-2text.php

Body-first consensus from reviewed literature:
- Primary racket-speed contributors: axial rotation of pelvis and trunk; shoulder
  horizontal adduction and internal rotation; grip style modulates swing plane and speed.
- End-point consistency: elbow/wrist angular positions at impact relatively stable across
  swings; path to impact may vary (supports variable practice for advanced players).
- Novice vs elite practice: blocked practice helps early skill; random/game-like practice
  benefits after baseline proficiency (pedagogy—not a joint angle).
- Scaled courts/balls increase successful forehand contacts and engagement in children
  (Farrow & Reid cited within review)—supports youth/adult separation.

Authority B+ | Landlinger et al. 2010 forehand timing (PMC)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3761808/

- Elite vs high-performance: later peak pelvis and trunk angular velocities before
  impact (~18 ms and ~15 ms differences reported) linked to higher shoulder/racket
  horizontal speeds (tendency, not prescription).
- Stance: open, closed, square, semi-open categories affect foot/hip alignment; elite
  match-play stance ratios vary by surface and player (limited sample case studies in
  Reid review).

Lambrich 2023 (above): groundstroke biomechanics comparisons similarly method-sensitive;
  open vs closed vs neutral stance kinematics not unified across studies.

6. SUGGESTED PHASE EVENTS FOR BODY TRACKING (DRAFT — BRAIN AGENT TO FINALIZE)
================================================================================

Overhead serve (align to Kovacs 8-stage where possible):
  START → BALL_RELEASE → LOAD_PEAK → TROPHY / COCKING → FORWARD_SWING →
  ARM_CONTACT_EST (proxy: max shoulder internal rotation / elbow extension peak) →
  DECEL → FINISH

Forehand:
  SPLIT / UNIT_TURN → BACKSWING_PEAK → FORWARD_SWING → IMPACT_PROXY → RECOVERY

Backhand (two-hand vs one-hand): separate phase models later; insufficient unified
  review coverage in this pass.

Footwork between shots:
  Reid review notes ~70% of elite groundstrokes involve ~3–4 m movement (notation data)—
  product can cue “small adjustment steps” vs “deep lunge” from pelvis displacement.
