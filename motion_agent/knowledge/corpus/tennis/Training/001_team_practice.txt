# Tennis Sport Pack — Team practice and training context

Educational summaries for coaches and captains. **Not** a replacement for certified coaching curricula or medical
return-to-play plans. Body-first focus aligns with CeRebel clips of serve and groundstroke mechanics.

---

## Practice block structure (adult recreational / high school)

Typical 90-minute court session split (B coaching convention):

1. **Dynamic warm-up (10 min):** Movement prep — lateral shuffles, leg swings, shoulder circles. No static-only stretching as sole warm-up.
2. **Activation (10 min):** Split-step hops, shadow groundstrokes, slow serve shadows emphasizing S_TROPHY_LOAD → S_DECEL.
3. **Technical theme (25 min):** One body theme per day — e.g., leg drive on serve, unit turn on forehand — tied to finding IDs.
4. **Live ball / rally (30 min):** Apply theme in cooperative rally; video one rep per athlete for CeRebel review if allowed.
5. **Conditioning or games (10 min):** Low-impact footwork patterns; avoid maximal serve volume when fatigued.
6. **Cool-down (5 min):** Easy movement, hydration.

**Load note:** High serve repetition when athletes show fault_serve_poor_decel or arm-dominant serve patterns increases
shoulder demand in coaching literature — rotate themes and monitor complaint of pain (refer to clinician).

## Youth practice (10U / red-orange-green)

**audience=youth**

- Shorter blocks (45–60 min) with frequent water and skill games.
- Court and ball scaling per USTA red/orange/green or Tennis Australia Hot Shots — smaller court reduces running load,
  lower compression ball slows rally for technical learning.
- Serve introduction via **throwing progressions** and abbreviated motion before full trophy-loop serve (see
  drill_youth_serve_throw_progression in Coaching/002_drills.txt).
- Avoid adult-style second-serve repetition drills for pre-pubescent athletes in one session; volume caps are coach
  judgment informed by NGB resources, not this pack alone.

**Sources:** usta_national_campus_youth (B); tennis_australia_hot_shots (B).

## Team captain / clinic workflow with CeRebel

1. Capture side-view serve or forehand during practice — consistent camera height at hip to chest level.
2. Trim clip to one stroke (S_READY through S_DECEL or G_SPLIT through G_RECOVERY if visible).
3. Review top finding cards; assign one cue + one 60s drill from Coaching/ for next practice station.
4. Re-shoot after two weeks to compare **same athlete** knee load and decel smoothness — not pro templates.

## Serve volume awareness (non-prescriptive)

Competitive players accumulate high serve counts in match and practice. Federation and sport-science materials recommend:
- Structured rest between hard serving days for developing athletes.
- Technique emphasis when introducing new motion changes — avoid simultaneous volume spikes.

This pack does **not** publish numeric serve caps (unlike baseball pitch counts); refer to national governing body
periodization for juniors and team schedule design.

**Sources:** ITF junior development articles (B+); PMC overuse reviews (A themes).

## Doubles vs singles footwork (body-only)

Doubles emphasizes ready position and split-step at net; singles emphasizes lateral recovery after G_RECOVERY.
Phase model G_SPLIT and G_RECOVERY checkpoints apply to both; net volley phases are future pack extension.

---

## Indoor / outdoor adjustments

Slippery indoor courts: wider base at G_LOAD may be adaptive — fault_groundstroke_wide_stance_stuck distinguishes
wide **without** knee flex from purposeful stability. Hot outdoor: fatigue shows as fault_double_fault_rhythm_rush;
coach may shorten sessions.

## Inclusive coaching notes

- Left-handed players mirror all sided cues in Coaching/001_cues.txt.
- Athletes with mobility limits may use abbreviated backswings while still passing deceleration_control checks.
- Wheelchair tennis uses different phase timing — not covered in v1 ontology; do not apply standing checkpoints.
