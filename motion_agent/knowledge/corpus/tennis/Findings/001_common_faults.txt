# Tennis — Common Faults (finding cards)
# Authority: B / B+ open biomechanics + NGB coaching summaries. Not diagnosis.
# See Docs/002_DISCLAIMER.txt. Body-first; contact = wrist-speed proxy when no ball track.

---

finding_id: fault_serve_knee_lock
headline_en: Serve loads with straight legs — power coming mostly from the arm
phases: S_TROPHY_LOAD, S_DRIVE_UP
body_signal: Knee flexion at S_TROPHY_LOAD similar to S_READY; little knee extension before S_CONTACT_PROXY; racket-side wrist speed peak still present (arm-dominant serve).
why_it_matters: Open biomechanics literature links leg drive to trunk rotation on the serve; arm-only patterns often coincide with higher shoulder demand and inconsistent pace for many recreational players (performance/load awareness, not injury prediction).
feel_cue: "Sit into a slight squat before you throw the racket up — push the ground first."
drill: 60s shadow serves holding a light squat at trophy, then explode up on one counted “up” (no ball required).
severity_default: medium
sources: PMC tennis serve biomechanics reviews (B+); USTA player development leg-drive coaching themes (B)
youth_note: Use smaller squat amplitude than adult elite models; prioritize rhythm over depth.

finding_id: fault_serve_toss_arm_drift
headline_en: Toss arm drifts sideways away from the body at the trophy load
phases: S_TOSS_RISE, S_TROPHY_LOAD
body_signal: Non-racket wrist lateral offset from midline increases at S_TROPHY_LOAD; racket-side shoulder may counter-lean; arm timing looks disconnected.
why_it_matters: Disorganized toss-arm path often disrupts serve rhythm and shoulder alignment cues used in NGB coaching (B); body-only check cannot score toss height without ball.
feel_cue: "Lift the front arm straight up like an elevator, then let it fall as the racket side goes up."
drill: 45s toss-arm-only reps: non-racket arm up-down while racket hand stays on hip; then 45s both arms without racket.
severity_default: medium
sources: ITF coaching articles on serve preparation (B+); LTA serve fundamentals summaries (B)
youth_note: Serve rhythm and recovery over trophy depth vs tour players.

finding_id: fault_serve_poor_leg_sync
headline_en: Legs extend after the arm has already peaked
phases: S_DRIVE_UP, S_CONTACT_PROXY
body_signal: Knee extension onset after racket-side wrist speed is already near peak; ankle push-off lags S_CONTACT_PROXY by more than a short adult band.
why_it_matters: Proximal-to-distal sequencing summaries associate late leg drive with arm-dominant loading on the serve (B+ performance/load framing).
feel_cue: "Push the floor first — the racket rides the legs up."

severity_default: medium
sources: PMC kinetic-chain serve reviews (B+); USTA adult development notes (B)
youth_note: Adult checkpoint primarily; youth focus on visible knee bend before upward swing, not elite delay numbers.

feel_cue: "Throw the racket into your opposite pocket — let the arm slow across your body."

feel_cue: "Step into the court on a soft front foot — land like a gymnast stick, then recover."

severity_default: medium

finding_id: fault_serve_short_finish_youth
headline_en: Abbreviated follow-through after contact proxy (youth volume awareness)

why_it_matters: Coaching frameworks encourage complete but controlled finishes for juniors while managing overhead volume (B); informational flag — not a medical stop.

finding_id: fault_serve_abbreviated_drop
headline_en: Racket drop skipped — arm pushes forward from trophy without a back loop

feel_cue: "Scratch your back lightly, then throw up — feel the loop before the lift."

severity_default: low
sources: ITF serve path progressions (B); serve kinematics reviews noting drop variability (B+)
confidence_note: Some high-level abbreviated serves are intentional; score vs athlete baseline, not pro template.
youth_note: Serve rhythm and recovery over trophy depth vs tour players.
