# Tennis Sport Pack — Biomechanics overview (open literature summary)

Summarized for CeRebel RAG from peer-reviewed reviews and federation sport-science materials.
**Not prescriptive medicine.** Reported relationships describe population trends with large individual variation.

Authority mix: B+ biomechanics reviews; A themes for overuse epidemiology; B coaching application.

## Serve: segment sequencing

Reviews of tennis serve biomechanics commonly describe energy transfer from lower body to upper body in proximal-to-distal
order: leg extension and force production against the ground, pelvis and trunk rotation, upper arm motion, forearm and
wrist action near the overhead hit window. Elite servers show diverse trophy poses and timing; research emphasizes
**coordination and repeatability** more than a single joint-angle template.

**Body-visible implications for CeRebel:**
- Leg contribution should precede peak racket-side wrist speed (S_DRIVE_UP before S_CONTACT_PROXY).
- Trunk tilt and rotation are visible on side/rear cameras; internal shoulder rotation is less reliable from one view.
- Non-racket arm motion contributes to balance and timing even when ball toss is not tracked.

**Sources:** PMC-indexed serve biomechanics review articles (search: tennis serve kinematics kinetics); ITF Coaching and
Sport Science Review (https://www.itftennis.com/en/about-us/governance/coaching-and-sport-science/).

## Serve: ground reaction forces and stability

Open studies report substantial vertical and horizontal ground reaction forces during the serve, with shift toward the
front foot into the follow-through. Platform vs pinpoint stance styles alter footwork timing but both require organized
load before drive. Product does not measure force plates; **proxy** uses knee flexion/extension and ankle rise timing.

**Camera note:** Without force data, avoid claiming “insufficient push” — use qualitative leg_contribution dimension.

## Serve: shoulder and elbow kinetics (awareness only)

Kinetic analyses show high shoulder and elbow loads at the competitive serve, especially with high volume. Epidemiology
links repetitive overhead activity to shoulder and elbow overuse conditions in tennis populations — multifactorial
(volume, technique, strength, recovery). **A single video clip cannot assess cumulative load or diagnose pathology.**

CeRebel may flag harsh deceleration or arm-dominant sequencing as **coaching and awareness** cues, not injury forecasts.

**Sources:** PMC tennis injury reviews; clinical consensus on gradual return to serve after pain (seek clinician).

## Groundstroke: trunk rotation and racket speed

Forehand and backhand studies report contributions from hip and trunk rotation to racket head speed. Open-stance forehands
increase trunk rotation demand compared with closed stance in some analyses; two-handed backhands involve coupled arm
actions with different shoulder loading patterns than one-handed backhands.

**Body-visible implications:**
- Unit turn (G_UNIT_TURN) should precede peak forward wrist speed for coordinated strokes in coaching models.
- “All-arm” swings show arm speed without preceding trunk yaw on side view — maps to fault_groundstroke_all_arm.

**Sources:** Open-access tennis groundstroke biomechanics papers (B+); USTA/LTA technique frameworks (B).

## Youth vs adult biomechanics

Junior players use scaled courts and balls (red/orange/green) with lower absolute forces and smaller segment amplitudes.
Growth and coordination limit copying adult professional kinematics. Training emphasis: throwing-like serve progressions,
wide base balance, and volume control per NGB guidelines — not maximizing wrist speed in isolation.

**Sources:** USTA red ball progression; Tennis Australia Hot Shots; ITF junior competition context (A+ rules, B training).

## Measurement limits with pose estimation

Joint angles from monocular video inherit error especially for:
- Shoulder internal/external rotation when arm is overhead.
- Wrist pronation/supination without racket marker.
- Pelvis twist when clothing is loose.

CeRebel findings should downgrade confidence when key joints are occluded or FPS < 30.

**Method reference:** OpenSim / musculoskeletal modeling literature (B) for future inverse kinematics pipelines.

## What we deliberately omit

- Exact degree norms for elbow flexion at trophy pose (too variable; risk of false precision).
- Ball spin mechanics (requires ball or racket sensor).
- String-bed impact physics.
