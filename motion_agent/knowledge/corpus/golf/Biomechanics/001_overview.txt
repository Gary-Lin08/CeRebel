## 1. What the literature agrees on (high level)

Full-swing performance in research is often studied through **kinematics** (segment rotations, joint angles, timing of peak
speeds) and **kinetics** (ground reaction force, centre of pressure, inverse-dynamics estimates). Both open-access reviews
stress that golf is highly individual, yet recurring themes appear:

1. **Proximal-to-distal sequencing** — Lower body and trunk contributions typically precede peak speeds farther out the chain
   in skilled patterns; transition timing (lower body reversing while upper body completes backswing) is a coaching and
   research focus.
2. **Pelvis–thorax separation (X-factor language)** — Dissociation between pelvis and shoulder girdle rotation is widely
   discussed, but **definitions and peak timing differ across studies**, limiting direct comparison of absolute degrees.
3. **Ground interaction** — Vertical and horizontal GRF components and CoP paths associate with clubhead speed and skill in
   multiple investigations; **timing of force application** is emphasized alongside magnitude.
4. **Methodological caution** — Heterogeneous marker sets, 2D vs 3D choices, and non-ISB joint definitions explain contradictory
   findings; product series should stay **internally consistent** rather than mixing published absolute norms.

CeRebel maps these themes to observable pose series (pelvis_rotation, thorax_rotation, separation, com_ap_shift,
hand_speed_lead, spine_flexion) — not to force-plate or launch-monitor ground truth.

## 2. Kinematics themes (Bourgain et al. 2022 — summary)

**Scope of review:** Systematic review of golf-swing **kinematics** literature (92 articles), categorized into X-factor,
crunch factor, swing plane / clubhead trajectory, kinematic sequence, and joint angular kinematics. Male golfers dominated
included studies; performance and injury motives both appear in the field.

**X-factor and separation:**
- X-factor denotes shoulder–pelvis dissociation; variants include **X-factor stretch** (peak separation early in downswing)
  and impact-phase definitions. McLean-style planar projections differ from 3D anatomical approaches.
- Reported links to clubhead speed exist in some cohorts, but **method choice shifts measured values** (e.g., thorax vs
  shoulder line, timing of peak). Joyce et al. and related work caution that comparing X-factor across studies without
  harmonized definitions is unreliable.
- **Coaching translation:** Use **relative** separation trends in the product (thorax_rotation − pelvis_rotation) and
  collapse-before-impact_proxy patterns — not a single “correct” degree for all players.

**Kinematic sequence:**
- Literature describes ordering of peak segment angular velocities (pelvis → thorax → arm → hand/club). Detection depends on
  which body segments and velocity components are analyzed (Philippot et al. — component effects on sequence identification).
- Bourgain et al. note **lack of consensus** preventing broad generalization; contradictory results partly reflect definitions
  and filtering of angular velocity signals.
- **Coaching translation:** Qualitative “lower body leads” / “transition stagger” cues; checkpoint cp_transition_lower uses
  pelvis_rot_vel vs hand_speed_lead ordering as a **proxy**, not lab peak timestamps from 2D pose.

**Crunch factor, swing plane, joint angles:**
- Crunch factor and lumbar loading appear in injury-related kinematics discussions; this overview does **not** translate
  epidemiology into personal injury risk scores in the app.
- Swing plane and clubhead trajectory domains are club-path heavy — glossary only for v1 body-first coaching.
- Joint-angle tables in the review illustrate **example** values from selected papers; ISB-aligned reporting is recommended
  for future research but is **not uniformly present** in legacy studies.

**Methodological recommendations (review authors):**
- Prefer **3D** approaches over 2D for rotation-dominated variables where possible.
- Follow **International Society of Biomechanics (ISB)** anatomical frames when reporting joint angles to enable aggregation.
- Report marker sets, filtering, and event definitions (top of backswing, impact) explicitly.

**Related DOIs in manifest (methods context, not duplicated here):**
- X-factor measurement: https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391003792603 (Joyce et al.)
- Sequence methods: https://doi.org/10.5277/abb-01318-2019-02 (Philippot et al.)
- Elite sequence example: https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2010.535842 (Zheng et al.)

## 3. Ground reaction force and CoP themes (Watson et al. 2026 — summary)

**Scope of review:** PRISMA 2020 systematic review of **GRF and CoP** during the golf swing and associations with
**clubhead speed (CHS)** and **skill level** (24 included studies).

**Key findings (population level):**
- Multiple empirical studies report **moderate-to-strong associations** between CHS and GRF and/or CoP metrics.
- **More skilled golfers** tended to show **higher GRF** (magnitude) and **better CHS** than less skilled cohorts in
  aggregated narrative synthesis — skill and swing style moderate any single metric.
- **Timing** of force development matters: effective use of GRF is described as supporting proximal-to-distal energy transfer,
  not merely producing maximum vertical force at an arbitrary instant.
- **CoP / weight transfer:** CoP is the application point of resultant horizontal GRF components; it is related to but not
  identical to centre-of-mass motion. Novice patterns often show incomplete shift toward the lead side before impact in
  coaching-oriented descriptions; experienced golfers more often exhibit fuller transfer — with study-level variability.
- Research field needs **standardized terminology** and reporting for GRF/CoP metrics across swing phases.

**CeRebel interpretation (estimated, qualitative):**
- Product checkpoints use **com_ap_shift**, **com_lateral**, and qualitative grf_vertical_est trends — **not** Newton targets
  from force-plate cohorts.
- Do **not** display Watson review magnitudes or handicap–CHS correlations as user pass/fail thresholds without external
  measurement tags.
- CHS and handicap statistics in the review contextualize **why** ground interaction is studied; they are **out of scope**
  for v1 body-only findings.

**Public coaching alignment (B sources — paraphrase only, see SOURCE_MANIFEST):**
- PGA / TPI / Titleist public articles emphasize AP shear, lead-foot push timing, and rotation — consistent with “timing +
  coordination” rather than bracing maximally at address.

## 5. Adult vs youth population notes

**Adults (B+ literature bands):**
- Separation and sequencing language in findings assumes full skeletal maturity and typical full-swing coaching context.
- GRF/CoP skill differences in Watson et al. largely reflect **adult male-dominated** study populations — do not infer junior
  numeric bands from these reviews.

**Youth (B / coaching consensus — separate audience tag):**
- Prioritize **balance, controlled rotation amplitude, and finish stability** over speed or “max ground force” cues.
- Shorter backswing depth when overswing threatens balance (finding overswing-depth in brain_plan).
- Weekly volume and multi-sport load follow NGB / club medical staff — see Training/001_team_practice.txt.
- **Never** apply adult CHS or GRF cohort statistics to junior-facing checkpoint copy.

## 6. Limitations block (mandatory for RAG consumers)

1. **Monocular depth error** affects lateral sway, AP shift, and estimated kinetics.
2. **Estimated GRF ≠ force plate** — direction/magnitude trends only, authority capped for checkpoints.
3. **X-factor / separation** definitions in literature ≠ internal series unless explicitly calibrated.
4. **No ball or club track** — clubhead speed, face, path, AoA remain glossary or future sensor ingest.
5. **Injury content** in full reviews is for researcher context; app outputs are coaching awareness, not diagnosis.
6. **Copyright:** Summaries only in this file; long-form CC BY review text remains in sibling Biomechanics/00*_*.txt files.
