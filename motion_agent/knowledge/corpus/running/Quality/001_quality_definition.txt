# Running Gait — Quality Definition (body-first)

## What “good running form” means in CeRebel

Quality is **not** a single score in v1. The product describes how well visible stride mechanics align with
checkpoints derived from open biomechanics and coaching literature — without claiming optimal form for every
individual or race distance.

Quality is evaluated in five dimensions. Each dimension links to checkpoints in `002_checkpoints.txt` and may
trigger finding IDs when failed.

### Dimension 1 — Contact and stride length

**Intent**: Foot arrives near the body’s center of mass with acceptable braking, not excessively ahead of the pelvis.

Body signals:
- Horizontal foot–pelvis relationship at contact proxy (overstride_proxy)
- Cadence and stride time consistency across cycles
- Stance duration proportion of stride period

Failed checkpoints may trigger: `overstride`, `low_cadence_overstride`, `stride_asymmetry`.

Evidence band: B+ (braking impulse and step-rate literature); coaching heuristics B.

### Dimension 2 — Posture and trunk control

**Intent**: Trunk supports stable head and pelvis alignment; neither collapsing backward nor hunching excessively forward.

Body signals:
- Trunk angle vs vertical in sagittal view at midstance and propulsion
- Head position relative to shoulders (when landmarks stable)

Failed checkpoints may trigger: `trunk_lean_back`, `trunk_lean_excess_fwd`.

Evidence band: B+ for energy cost and ground reaction force themes; B for cueing.

### Dimension 3 — Hip and pelvis stability

**Intent**: Pelvis stays relatively level during single-leg support; knees track without extreme inward collapse on
visible camera plane.

Body signals:
- Pelvis obliquity at midstance (hip_drop)
- Frontal-plane knee angle proxy when camera allows (knee_valgus_load)
- Crossover: foot medial to contralateral hip line (crossover_gait)

Failed checkpoints may trigger: `hip_drop`, `knee_valgus_load`, `crossover_gait`.

Evidence band: A/B+ for hip stabilizer weakness themes in literature; product thresholds conservative (B+).

### Dimension 4 — Arm swing and trunk rotation

**Intent**: Arms counterbalance leg motion with roughly symmetric range; trunk rotation supports forward drive
without excessive twist.

Body signals:
- Shoulder flexion/extension ROM per cycle, left vs right
- Thorax yaw amplitude relative to pelvis

Failed checkpoints may trigger: `arm_swing_asymmetry`, `excessive_trunk_rotation`.

Evidence band: B+ for arm swing and metabolic cost; B for asymmetry coaching.

### Dimension 5 — Rhythm and repeatability

**Intent**: Stride timing and key angles repeat across cycles unless fatigue narrative is explicit.

Body signals:
- Coefficient of variation of stride period left and right
- Drift in vertical oscillation or cadence over clip

Failed checkpoints may trigger: `stride_asymmetry`, `excessive_bounce` when combined with high oscillation variance.

Evidence band: B coaching; B+ for variability-injury association themes (non-diagnostic).

## Youth vs adult quality interpretation

Youth:
- Higher cadence and shorter contact times are normal.
- Mild asymmetry may appear during growth; down-weight severity unless large or paired with pain language.

Adult:
- Apply adult checkpoint bands in 002; masters runners may show longer contact times — product may add
  `audience=masters` in future without changing phase IDs.

## Relationship to findings severity

Checkpoints produce **coaching priorities**, not medical grades. A failed hip-drop checkpoint suggests
stability work and film review; it does not diagnose glute med pathology.

## Repeatability and capture quality

Quality analysis confidence drops when:
- FPS < 24, heavy motion blur, or occluded ankles
- Camera not approximately sagittal for overstride/trunk claims
- Clip shorter than two stride cycles per leg

Document low-confidence state in coach briefs (product logic); KB defines the concepts only.
