# Running Gait Biomechanics — Overview (evidence-aware summary)

This document summarizes **open-access and widely cited research themes** for CeRebel body-first coaching.
It does not prescribe individual medical treatment or guarantee injury prevention. Numeric values are
**cohort ranges** from literature — not universal targets for every runner.

Authority key: A+ federation/clinical consensus | A peer-reviewed clinical | B+ strong reviews |
B coaching consensus | ProductLogic internal measurement definitions.

---

## 1. Stride cycle and phases (ProductLogic + B+)

Running differs from walking by a **flight phase** when neither foot supports body weight. One stride cycle
for a leg spans initial contact through loading, midstance, propulsion, toe-off, swing, and flight until the
next contact on that leg.

CeRebel maps phases to contact proxies from ankle kinematics (see Phases/001). Force-plate initial contact
and video-based proxies can differ by tens of milliseconds — product copy must say **contact proxy**.

---

## 2. Cadence and stride length (B+)

**Cadence** (steps per minute, spm) and **stride length** trade off at a given speed. Many recreational
runners self-select cadences below elite distance runners, but wide healthy variation exists.

Heiderscheit et al. (2011) manipulated step rate ±5–10% at constant speed and reported changes in hip and
knee joint work and loading metrics — supporting that **increasing step rate can reduce some peak loading
variables** without changing speed in controlled lab conditions. Authority: **B+**. This is not proof that
every runner should increase cadence; abrupt changes can alter tissue stress elsewhere.

Coaching implication (B): when overreaching is visible **and** cadence is low for context, consider gradual
turnover cues rather than isolated “foot strike” changes.

**Youth**: children often use higher step rates and shorter strides relative to height; adult cadence bands
should not be applied to U12 without widened tolerances (authority B+, developmental gait literature themes).

---

## 3. Overstriding and braking (B+)

**Overstriding** describes foot contact occurring noticeably **ahead** of the body’s center of mass (often
estimated via pelvis or hip position in sagittal view). Biomechanics literature associates this pattern with
**greater braking impulse** and vertical impact peaks in some cohorts — authority **B+** (multiple kinematic
studies and reviews; not causal injury proof in all populations).

CeRebel uses **overstride_proxy**: horizontal foot–pelvis relation at contact proxy, normalized when possible
by leg segment length. Monocular scale error limits absolute centimeter claims.

Foot-strike label (rearfoot vs midfoot) is **not** reliably determined from side-view 2D alone when ankle
rotation is occluded — avoid diagnostic foot-strike coaching from low-confidence pose.

---

## 4. Vertical oscillation and running economy (B+)

**Vertical oscillation** is the vertical displacement of the center of mass or pelvis each stride. Excessive
bounce may increase energy cost in some runners (authority **B+** for economy relationships; individual
optima vary by speed and anatomy).

Product approach: flag high oscillation relative to **self-baseline** across strides in the same clip when
possible, rather than a single universal centimeter norm.

---

## 5. Trunk posture and lean (B+ / B)

Distance runners often display **slight forward lean** from the ankle with a relatively stable trunk segment
during midstance. Excessive upright trunk or apparent **leaning back** at contact can coincide with overreach
and braking in coaching analysis (B+ themes in form reviews; B for cue language).

**Hunching** forward from the waist (flexed thoracic posture) is a separate pattern from ankle-driven lean;
address with posture cues and mobility work in supervised settings (B coaching).

---

## 6. Hip drop and frontal-plane control (A/B+)

**Pelvis obliquity** during stance — swing-side hip appearing lower — is discussed in clinical and
biomechanical literature in relation to hip abductor function and iliotibial band **symptoms** in some
studies. Authority **A/B+** for association themes; **not** a diagnosis from video alone.

Measurement: best with rear or frontal camera; sagittal-only capture downgrades confidence for hip_drop findings.

**Knee valgus** (dynamic knee inward motion) during loading appears in ACL and overuse **risk factor**
research for cutting sports; in straight-line running, visible valgus may still matter for load distribution
(B+). Product uses conservative visible proxy only when frontal/oblique camera supports it.

---

## 7. Crossover gait (B+)

Feet landing **crossing the midline** (narrow base or “cross-over”) appear in some injured or fatigued
runners in clinical observational reports (B/B+). Rear-view capture improves detection. May relate to hip
stability and trail-leg path — coaching focuses on hip width and foot placement under hip, not aggressive
manual foot forcing on treadmill.

---

## 8. Arm swing and trunk rotation (B+ / B)

Arm swing counterbalances leg rotation and may reduce **torsional load** on the trunk. Asymmetric arm carriage
can reflect compensation for leg injury history, handheld items, or habit (B coaching). Bilateral ROM comparison
from shoulder landmarks is a product heuristic — not a full joint moment analysis.

**Trunk rotation** in the transverse plane is normal; excessive amplitude relative to pace may correlate with
inefficiency in some coaching frameworks (B). Do not pathologize moderate rotation in sprinting vs distance.

---

## 9. Stance compliance: knee and ankle (B+)

During **loading**, knee flexion increases to absorb impact. Very **stiff** landing appearance (minimal knee
flexion, stiff ankle) may increase impact peaks in some lab studies (B+). Conversely, excessive sinking without
propulsion may indicate fatigue or strength limits (B coaching).

Ankle **dorsiflexion** at contact varies with strike pattern and shoe heel drop — 2D pose error is large;
stiff_ankle_landing finding ships at lower confidence unless clear in high-FPS sagittal video.

---

## 10. Asymmetry (B+)

Left–right differences in **contact time**, stride length proxy, or joint ROM are common. Persistent large
asymmetry may warrant coach review and clinical screening if pain present (A clinical consensus for pain,
not for angle alone). Youth growth may produce temporary asymmetry (B+ developmental themes).

Product threshold: stride time asymmetry roughly **>8–10%** sustained across multiple cycles triggers
stride_asymmetry finding (conservative heuristic, authority B+).

## 11. OpenSim / body model coordinate glossary (ProductLogic)

Series IDs used across Quality and Findings:

| series_id | Typical coordinate / computation |
|-----------|----------------------------------|
| knee_flex_stance | knee flexion angle, stance leg |
| knee_flex_swing | swing leg knee flexion for clearance |
| trunk_lean | angle of thorax vs vertical in sagittal plane |
| hip_drop | difference in hip landmark height L vs R at midstance |
| trunk_rotation | thorax yaw relative to pelvis |
| arm_swing_range | max–min shoulder flexion/extension per cycle |
| overstride_proxy | ankle–pelvis horizontal offset at E_IC / leg length |
| vertical_oscillation | peak–peak pelvis vertical position per cycle |
| cadence | 60 / mean stride period from ic events |

Joint torques, ground reaction forces, and muscle activations are **not** computed in v1 body-only pipeline.

## 12. Limitations of monocular body tracking (ProductLogic)

- Scale and depth ambiguity affect stride length and overstride absolute values.
- Frontal-plane knee and hip metrics require appropriate camera angle.
- Fatigue within clip is not inferred unless cadence or ROM drift is modeled.
- Footwear, surface (treadmill vs road), and speed strongly change kinematics — context metadata recommended.

## 14. Run-likeness and sport mismatch (ProductLogic)

Future RunningPack detector expects **periodic bilateral ankle signals** and flight intervals. Golf swing,
baseball pitch, and tennis stroke patterns should not match run_likeness — pack refuses or falls back to
general human motion. KB does not embed golf or pitch mechanics.
