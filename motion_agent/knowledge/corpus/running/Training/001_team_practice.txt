# Running — Team Practice and Group Film Sessions

Body-first capture for teams (cross-country, track distance, running clubs) without GPS or timing gates.
Summarizes reputable coaching practice patterns (authority B); verify league rules and medical staff policies locally.

---

## 1. Session goals for motion analysis

- **Consistency**: film easy pace first — form changes at race pace.
- **Repeatability**: 6–10 strides in frame, stable camera, athlete in plain clothing when possible.
- **Context tag**: treadmill vs track vs road; shoe type; fatigue state (warm-up vs post-workout).

CeRebel briefs prioritize one or two findings per athlete per session — avoid overwhelming youth teams with
twelve simultaneous cues.

---

## 2. Warm-up structure (before filming)

Typical 10–15 minute movement prep (B coaching consensus):

1. Easy jog 5–8 minutes — do not film first minute (settling rhythm).
2. Dynamic mobility: leg swings (front/back, lateral), hip circles, ankle pumps.
3. Strides (4–6 × 15–20 s accelerations at ~mile effort) with walk-back recovery — optional second film clip
   at stride pace for comparison.

Youth: shorten strides count; emphasize play-based mobility for U12.

---

## 3. Camera setup for teams

**Side view (preferred for overstride and trunk lean)**:
- Camera perpendicular to path, hip height, 3–5 m from lane.
- Capture full body head to feet; avoid cropping feet at contact.

**Rear view (hip drop and crossover)**:
- Centered behind athlete; narrow track lane helps.

**Treadmill**:
- Side mount outside belt; note belt speed in session log.
- Overstride interpretation uses pelvis–foot relation in image (see Phases/001).

Multiple athletes: queue one at a time; keep background static for pose stability.

---

## 4. Using phase scrub in a team setting

Coach workflow (product UX target):

1. Review **clip-level** cadence and asymmetry flags first.
2. Scrub to **contact** and **midstance** for the highest-severity finding.
3. Assign **one** cue + **one** 30–90 s drill from Coaching/ for homework.
4. Re-film after 2–3 weeks at same pace and camera angle — compare qualitatively.

Avoid public call-outs of medical language; use performance and balance framing.

## 6. Weekly load and form work (pointers only)

Training volume and intensity are **coach- and clinician-governed**. This pack does not set mileage.

General pattern (B):

- **Base phase**: 1 short form-focused segment per easy run (e.g., 4 × 30 s cadence cue).
- **Competition phase**: maintain one maintenance drill weekly; avoid large mechanical changes pre-race.
- **Return from time off**: rebuild volume before aggressive cadence changes.

Youth: follow school/club policies on consecutive hard days; form film is educational, not load prescription.

Masters: longer contact times and reduced elasticity are common — widen asymmetry and cadence heuristics in
interpretation (document audience when product supports).

---

## 7. Partner and squad exercises

- **Mirror runs**: pairs side-by-side at easy pace — coach observes synchrony (not a product feature; film one runner).
- **Lane line drill**: run on painted line without crossing — rear camera checks crossover_gait (external focus B).
- **Hill repeats** (moderate grade): natural forward lean and knee drive; film downhill easy portion separately —
  mechanics differ from flats.

---

## 8. Indoor / winter constraints

Treadmill sessions useful for consistent camera placement; remind athletes that belt pull may alter foot
position relative to ground. Alternate with overground film when weather allows.

---

## 9. Safety and inclusion

- Stop session for pain, dizziness, or asthma symptoms — standard first aid and staff protocols.
- Para-athletes and prosthetic limbs require individualized coaching; default checkpoints may not apply —
  human review mandatory.

---

## 10. Parent communication (youth clubs)

Share Docs/002_DISCLAIMER short copy. Emphasize: app findings are **coaching observations**, not medical
screening replacing pre-participation physicals where required by state or federation.
