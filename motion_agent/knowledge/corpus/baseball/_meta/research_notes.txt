1. CONSENSUS PHASE MODELS (RECONCILE IN brain_plan / Phases/)
================================================================================

1A. ASMI / clinical six-phase model (primary for body tracking events)
  Source: Diffendaffer et al., Sports Health 2023 (PMC9950989); lineage Fleisig/ASMI.
  Phases and boundary events:
    - Wind-up: first motion → peak lead-knee height (leg lift).
    - Stride: peak knee height → lead foot contact (first contact with ground).
    - Arm cocking: foot contact → maximum shoulder external rotation (MER / “layback”).
    - Arm acceleration: MER → ball release.
    - Arm deceleration: ball release → end of rapid arm slowdown.
    - Follow-through: until motion stops / fielding posture.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9950989/

1B. OBP processing events (align detectors when force data unavailable)
  Source: OBP baseball_pitching README (GitHub).
  Named events in full-signal tables:
    foot contact (~10% bodyweight on lead leg),
    foot plant (~100% bodyweight),
    maximum external rotation,
    ball release,
    maximum internal rotation.
  URL: https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/blob/main/baseball_pitching/README.md
  Note: “Foot contact” ≠ “foot plant” in lab nomenclature; Driveline assessments also distinguish
    foot contact vs foot plant (PMC-adjacent coaching: Driveline assessment blog).

1C. Driveline 12-subphase sequencing model (coaching granularity, overlaps allowed)
  Source: “Sequencing the 95 MPH Delivery” (Driveline blog, 2014).
  Lower half: Loading → Striding → Rotation → Blocking
  Trunk: Rotation → Stabilization → Flexion
  Arm: Separation → Pickup → Disconnection → Driveline → Recovery
  Reference still frames cited: balance point, stride foot contact, MER, release, MIR.
  URL: https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/sequencing-the-95-mph-delivery

1D. Baseball Canada coaching phases (youth NGB, qualitative)
  Grip → Stance → Wind-up / Set → Gather → Stride → Landing & Release → Follow-through
  URL: https://www.baseball.ca/pitching-mechanics

2. KINEMATIC NORMS & KEY NUMBERS (ADULT / LAB — NOT PRESCRIPTIVE MEDICAL TARGETS)
================================================================================

Treat as population references from 3D mocap literature; monocular body tracking will have
 wider tolerance bands. Separate youth vs adult in product copy.

At lead foot contact (Diffendaffer / ASMI clinical guide, PMC9950989):
  - Stride length ≈ 85% of body height (distance hind ankle at max knee → lead ankle at contact).
  - Lead foot: slightly closed position and closed toe angle (RHP toward 3B side / closed).
  - Lead knee flexion ≈ 45°.
  - Pelvis rotated open toward plate ≈ 35°; shoulders still roughly parallel to target line
    (hip-shoulder separation / “closed upper body”).
  - Throwing elbow flexion ≈ 90°.
  - Shoulder abduction ≈ 90°, horizontal abduction ≈ 20°, external rotation ≈ 45°.
  Fault links: open lead foot → increased anterior shoulder & medial elbow force; late arm often
    with short stride; excessive horizontal abduction at contact → anterior capsule stress concern.

Arm cocking → MER:
  - Peak shoulder external rotation ≈ 170° (performance benefit but excessive MER linked to
    impingement risk and elevated elbow varus torque in literature cited therein).

Arm acceleration → ball release:
  - Peak elbow extension angular velocity up to ~2700°/s; shoulder IR velocity up to ~7500°/s
    (lab reports; not measurable directly in CeRebel MVP).
  - At release: shoulder abduction ~90°; elbow flexion ~25°; trunk flexed toward target ~35°;
    contralateral trunk tilt ~20° (glove-side lean).
  - Lead knee flexion ~30° at release (extends from ~45° at contact); insufficient lead-knee
    extension linked to lower ball velocity in cited studies.

Follow-through:
  - Elbow rebounds to ~45° flexion; fielding-ready posture.

Japanese vs American pro comparison (Oi et al., PMC6512154, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0):
  - Illustrates population/style variance: stride length, shoulder abduction at release, knee
    flexion after release differ between cohorts; normalize by body size when comparing.

OBP POI distribution (research norms — CC BY-NC-SA, research use only):
  - 411 FF trials; median pitch speed 85.3 mph (69.5–94.4); mostly college.
  - POI fields include: stride_length (% height), hip-shoulder separation at foot plant,
    MER, elbow/shoulder angles at foot plant and release, timing_peak_torso_to_peak_pelvis_rot_velo,
    peak segment angular velocities, elbow varus moment, energy flow metrics.
  - Use for internal percentile bands, not consumer-facing “medical thresholds” without validation.
  URLs: https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/blob/main/DATASHEET.md
        https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/blob/main/baseball_pitching/README.md

3. KINETIC CHAIN & SEQUENCING (PERFORMANCE / LOAD FRAMING)
================================================================================

Proximal-to-distal sequencing (clinical consensus):
  From foot contact through release: lead knee extension → pelvis rotation → upper trunk rotation
  → elbow extension → shoulder internal rotation (Diffendaffer PMC9950989).
  Improper pelvis/trunk timing = common kinetic-chain flaw; core/hip mobility and strength interventions.

Driveline kinematic sequencing preference (coaching + lab reports):
  Peak angular velocities ideally ordered ground-up: Pelvis → Torso → Elbow → Shoulder.
  Pelvis-to-torso peak timing delta correlates with velocity in their internal database
  (example ~0.039 s cited as “pretty good” in blog; not universal constant).
  URL: https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/interpret-biomechanics-reports

Whole-body assessment emphasis:
  “There’s More to Assess than the Arm” (PMC11698006) — lower extremity and trunk drive upper
  extremity kinetics; clinical exam should include kinetic chain, not isolated arm care.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11698006/

Return-to-sport / analysis methods review (PMC8811517):
  Phases: wind-up, stride, arm cocking, acceleration, deceleration, follow-through.
  KPIs: shoulder/elbow torque, rotation, kinetic chain (trunk timing, hip-shoulder separation),
  stride characteristics; gold standard = marker-based 3D mocap; wearables/markerless emerging.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8811517/

Mechanics–injury relationships (review, PMC5435152):
  Velocity and fatigue as injury predictors; mechanics modulate shoulder/elbow torque; ties to
  Pitch Smart workload tables; hip-shoulder separation and hand-on-top themes in qualitative efficiency work.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5435152/

Deceleration-phase elbow varus moment (PMC6446515, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0):
  Non-trivial varus moment can persist after max internal rotation during deceleration (~half of
  peak acceleration phase for some pitchers); consider deceleration in arm-care framing (not diagnosis).
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6446515/

4. COMMON FAULTS → BODY SIGNALS → CORRECTIVE IDEAS (FOR Findings/ COACHING)
================================================================================

| Fault (headline) | Phase | Visible / measurable signal | Why it matters (careful wording) | Drill / intervention ideas | Primary sources |
|------------------|-------|-----------------------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------|-----------------|
| Open lead foot at landing | Stride / FC | Foot lands open to arm-side; pelvis opens early | Associated with altered pelvic rotation & higher shoulder/elbow loads in mocap studies | Line on mound; land 4–14" closed; closed toe; hip ROM/strength if cannot comply | PMC9950989 |
| Short stride / late arm | Stride / FC | Stride < expected; low ER at foot contact | Late arm linked to higher elbow/shoulder stress; less time for arm to set | Increase stride if hip abduction ROM allows; hand separation timing drills | PMC9950989 |
| Poor hip-shoulder separation timing | Cocking | Trunk rotates with pelvis; low separation at FC | Reduces energy transfer; timing flaw common | Med ball throws; half-kneel throws; separation-focused constraint drills | PMC9950989, Driveline sequencing blog |
| Excessive contralateral trunk lean at release | Acceleration | Glove-side lean ↑ | Elbow varus moment increases ~4 Nm per 10° in cited study | Trunk control exercises; adjust tilt not “arm slot” in isolation | Solomito 2015 via PMC9950989 refs |
| Insufficient lead knee extension | Acceleration | Lead knee stays flexed BR | Lower velocity in cited biomechanics studies | Fix foot plant stability first; quad strength; step-down tests | PMC9950989 |
| Early trunk / uphill tilt at FC | Cocking | Lead shoulder high; abduction >90° | Linked to excessive shoulder abduction at cocking | Level trunk at FC; thoracic mobility | PMC9950989 |
| Leading with elbow (horizontal adduction + flexion at MER) | Cocking | Elbow leads body at layback | Seen with reduced effort, fatigue, weakness | Full-chain strengthening; gradual intent progression | PMC9950989 |
| Early extension / rear hip leak | Loading/stride | COM drifts forward; rear foot not anchored | Limits glute-driven pelvis rotation (Driveline case example) | Drop steps, plyo constraint drills, “spread the floor” cue | Driveline assessment blog |
| Deceleration stress | Decel | Rapid elbow flexion rebound; continued varus loading | Additional load after release in subset of pitchers | Eccentric rotator cuff / decel drills (clinical guide lists examples) | PMC9950989, PMC6446515 |

Baseball Canada qualitative faults (youth coaching):
  - Guiding ball to plate (dart throw) vs free arm path.
  - Stiff locked front leg at landing (avoid).
  - Dragging pivot foot on follow-through.
  - Head pulling off line during stride.
  Drills: Drop step pivot gather; movement toward plate (hand break timing); balanced landing;
  long toss; two-knee / one-knee partner toss; lead arm barrier drill.
  URL: https://www.baseball.ca/pitching-mechanics

Driveline constraint / PlyoCare drill families (environment > verbose cueing):
  Reverse throws (always first), pivot picks, scap retraction, roll-ins, step backs, drop steps,
  janitors, rockers, walking windups; submax intent; wall throws to reduce partner anxiety.
  URLs: https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/plyo-velocity-weighted-balls-replication
        https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/different-model-cueing-pitching-mechanics
        https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/pitching-assessments-changing-mechanics

5. WORKLOAD & YOUTH (SEPARATE FROM MECHANICS FINDINGS)
================================================================================

Pitch Smart (MLB / USA Baseball):
  Age-based daily pitch limits and required rest days; avoid multiple games same day; limit
  months pitching per year; delay breaking pitches until ~13; avoid pitcher+catcher same game.
  URLs: https://www.mlb.com/pitch-smart/pitching-guidelines
        https://www.usabaseball.com/play/pitch-smart/

Empirical compliance study (PMC8581780, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0):
  Many youth behaviors violate Pitch Smart; reinforces need for workload messaging in app disclaimers.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8581780/

Baseball Canada pitch count guidelines (Canadian NGB):
  URL: https://www.baseball.ca/en/programs/development/pitch-count-guidelines

Little League official pitching rules:
  URL: https://www.littleleague.org/university/articles/pitch-counts/

ASMI youth position statement:
  URL: https://asmi.org/asmi-position-statement-for-youth-baseball-pitchers/

Youth mechanics systematic review (PMC5857730):
  MER in youth reported ~166–178° range in reviewed studies (do not apply adult 170° as single target).
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5857730/

7. SUGGESTED CeRebel DETECTOR EVENT MAP (PROVISIONAL)
================================================================================

Map monocular body phases to ASMI six-phase model:
  PEAK_KNEE_LIFT     → end wind-up
  FOOT_CONTACT       → start arm cocking (OBP: 10% BW; video: first flat-foot / max forward stride foot)
  MAX_SHOULDER_ER    → MER (proxy: throwing elbow behind body, forearm lag peak)
  RELEASE            → ball release proxy (trail wrist peak speed / hand forward extremum) — existing pitch_score
  MAX_INTERNAL_ROT   → post-release arm wrap
  MOTION_END         → follow-through settle

Validate against OBP timing fields only in non-commercial research setting.
