# Baseball Pitching — Biomechanics Overview (open literature summary)

This document summarizes **relationships** reported in open-access reviews and clinical consensus documents.
It does not prescribe universal angle targets for individual athletes. Camera-based apps measure proxies, not
lab-grade joint moments.

Primary sources: PMC3764822 (pitching biomechanics overview), ASMI public materials, PMC3445080 (workload/injury context).
Authority default: B+ for kinematic relationships; A for injury/workload themes at population level.

---

## Kinetic chain concept

Overhand pitching is a **proximal-to-distal** sequence: lower extremity generates force, trunk rotates and
tilts, throwing arm accelerates through a large range of motion, hand/wrist deliver final velocity to the ball.

Efficient sequencing (general pattern in literature):
1. Lead foot accepts force while pelvis rotates toward plate.
2. Trunk rotation peaks near arm acceleration.
3. Elbow extends and wrist speed peaks near release.

When trunk rotation peaks **before** the front leg firms, the arm often compensates — visible as early shoulder
opening or horizontal push. CeRebel maps this tendency to fault_early_trunk_rotation using yaw timing proxies.

## Segments and joints (body tracking view)

### Lower body
- **Drive leg** pushes against rubber; extension timing correlates with pelvis velocity in lab studies.
- **Stride leg** length and direction affect timing of pelvis rotation; excessive closed landing restricts hip rotation.
- **GRF** (ground reaction force) not measured in CeRebel v1; inferred indirectly via ankle/knee kinematics.

Visible proxies:
- Lead ankle forward velocity and deceleration at plant.
- Knee flexion stability through release.

### Trunk and pelvis
- **Pelvis rotation** toward plate begins during stride and continues through release.
- **Trunk rotation** often lags pelvis briefly — hip-shoulder **separation** — storing elastic energy in torso tissues
  when timing is appropriate (qualitative in coaching; quantitative in 3D labs).

2D limitation: oblique cameras blend rotation into flexion; separation metrics carry higher uncertainty — product uses
qualitative checkpoint language unless multi-view calibration exists.

### Throwing arm
- **Arm cocking (late cocking)**: throwing arm reaches maximum external rotation relative to trunk; elbow flexed.
  Lab studies associate this phase with high elbow valgus load — **population-level** finding, not individual diagnosis.
- **Acceleration**: rapid elbow extension and internal rotation; wrist flexion/extension contributes to release velocity in lab models.
- **Deceleration**: eccentric work of posterior shoulder and elbow musculature; incomplete follow-through discussed as
  a modifiable coaching factor over high cumulative workloads.

CeRebel does **not** estimate valgus moment from 2D pose.

## Timing variables (conceptual, not single norms)

Researchers often report group means for intervals such as:
- Foot contact to release
- Maximum pelvis rotation to release
- Maximum shoulder external rotation to release

These intervals vary by pitcher height, delivery style (stretch vs windup), and pitch type in lab settings with ball tracking.
Product approach: compare athlete to **their session baseline** rather than publishing fixed millisecond norms.

Release detection in product: **E_RELEASE = argmax trail wrist speed** (pitch_score.py).

---

## Stride mechanics

Stride length in research is often normalized to body height or leg length. Very short strides associated with
upright trunk and arm-dominant patterns in coaching literature; overstride associated with landing instability.

Direction: stride toward plate supports hip rotation; closed landing can force early trunk opening — mapped to
fault_landing_closed.

---

## Shoulder and elbow (health-aware coaching language)

Open clinical reviews emphasize:
- **Cumulative workload** (pitches per game, season, year) as primary modifiable risk factor for youth elbow/shoulder pain.
- **Mechanics** as secondary modifiable factor — especially timing, deceleration, and avoiding concurrent high-intensity
  throwing when fatigued.

ASMI youth position statement themes (summarized):
- Develop overall athleticism and throwing fundamentals before specialization.
- Avoid year-round maximal throwing without adequate rest.
- Do not treat one kinematic snapshot as clearance to throw through pain.

Product implication: pair mechanics findings with Pitch Smart / league rest tables for youth sessions.

---

## Wrist and hand

Late wrist flexion/extension contributes to release velocity in biomechanical models with force plates and high-speed video.
Without ball track, CeRebel uses **wrist speed burst** as efficiency proxy (fault_push_no_whip when burst low).

---

## Comparison to other throwing sports

Javelin, cricket, and tennis serves share proximal-to-distal sequencing but differ in plane of motion, stride constraints,
and object weight. Do not transfer golf swing or tennis serve norms to pitching phase model.

Golf misclassification is explicitly guarded in baseball pack (different plane, dual wrist peaks).

## Measurement quality factors

| Factor | Effect on inference |
|--------|---------------------|
| Camera side view | Best for arm path and trunk flexion |
| 30 fps vs 60 fps | Underestimates peak wrist speed |
| Single vs multi-camera | Rotation angles unreliable in one view |
| Markerless pose noise | Smooth signals before peak detection |

Recommend checkpoint cp_capture_quality before high-confidence findings.

---

## Youth vs adult biomechanics notes

Youth pitchers:
- Smaller absolute forces; growth plates sensitive to volume — prioritize Pitch Smart limits.
- Greater inter-athlete variability in maturity; avoid adult pro model comparisons.

Adult pitchers:
- Higher absolute joint loads; deceleration capacity trainable with S&C (general literature) but individualized.

No youth/adult split on phase IDs — split on workload policies and cue complexity.

---

## Open research gaps (do not overclaim in product)

- Direct causal links between single 2D pose fault labels and injury incidence are **not** established in literature.
- Pitch-type-specific kinematics require ball and grip context.
- Fatigue detection from one clip is speculative; multi-pitch drift analysis future work.
