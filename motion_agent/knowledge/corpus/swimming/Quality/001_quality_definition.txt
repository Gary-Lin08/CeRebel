# Freestyle — Quality Definition (body-first)

## What “quality” means in CeRebel

Quality is **not** a single split time or SWOLF score. For this pack, **quality freestyle** (movement quality) means:

1. **Stable body line** — hips near surface without excessive vertical oscillation; head neutral except brief breath window.
2. **Ordered pull sequence** — entry → catch with elbow above wrist proxy → accelerating pull → complete push past hip.
3. **Symmetric timing (when bilateral)** — left and right cycle periods and pull peaks within athlete baseline band.
4. **Integrated roll and kick** — trunk rotates with pull; kick originates from hip without bicycle knee action.
5. **Breathing without disrupting pull** — head rotates rather than lifts straight up; breath timed away from max force on breathing side when possible.
6. **Training-load context** — for youth, quality includes appropriate volume and skill progression, not only aesthetics.

Quality findings are **coaching signals**, not medical grades.

Authority mix: B+ biomechanics relationships, B NGB coaching progressions, A for pain/overuse language boundaries.

---

## Dimensions of quality (orthogonal axes)

### axis_catch_pull
Definition: Effective setup and propulsion from entry through push.
High quality: EVF proxy after entry; wrist speed peak mid-pull; exit near hip.
Low quality: dropped elbow; sculling outward; short push.

Measurable proxies:
- Elbow-wrist vertical ordering in side view during catch.
- Time from E_ENTRY to E_PULL_PEAK within athlete band.
- Wrist passes hip keypoint before E_EXIT.

### axis_body_line
Definition: Horizontal alignment and minimal snaking.
High quality: spine long; hips stable; entry at shoulder width.
Low quality: crossover entry; hips fishtail; legs spread wide.

Measurable proxies:
- Lateral wrist entry position vs shoulder midline (2D).
- Pelvis lateral displacement per cycle.

### axis_rotation
Definition: Coupled hip-shoulder roll supporting reach and pull.
High quality: roll peaks with pull; sufficient amplitude for reach without over-rotation.
Low quality: flat swim; excessive roll with crossover.

Measurable proxies:
- Shoulder tilt peak-to-peak per cycle.
- Phase lag between roll peak and E_PULL_PEAK.

### axis_breathing
Definition: Head motion during breath.
High quality: rotation-dominated breath; quick return to neutral.
Low quality: straight lift; breath during peak pull on same side.

Measurable proxies:
- Head vertical displacement during E_BREATHE.
- Yaw magnitude vs vertical displacement ratio.

### axis_kick
Definition: Flutter kick efficiency proxy.
High quality: narrow kick; hip-driven; ankles relaxed.
Low quality: bicycle kick; large splash; kick pauses.

Measurable proxies:
- Knee flexion amplitude.
- Ankle lateral separation.

### axis_repeatability
Definition: Consistency across cycles in clip.
High quality: low SD on cycle period and E_PULL_PEAK timing.
Low quality: high variance — fatigue, pain avoidance, or skill instability.

Requires ≥4 cycles.

### axis_workload_youth
Definition: Volume and intensity appropriate to age and skill stage.
High quality: follows NGB progression; technique focus before distance overload.
Low quality: excessive yardage with breakdown in catch/pull — flag coaching context.

Audience: youth; sources in Training/ and SOURCE_MANIFEST.

---

## Quality tiers (product language)

tier_excellent:
- No medium/high findings on catch_pull, body_line, rotation, breathing, or kick axes.
- Repeatability SD in lowest tertile of session history if available.

tier_good:
- At most one medium finding; no high-severity shoulder-load pattern flags.
- Asymmetry below configured threshold L/R.

tier_needs_work:
- Multiple medium findings OR one high finding (e.g., persistent dropped elbow both sides).

tier_insufficient_data:
- Fewer than 2 full cycles, heavy occlusion, or misclassified non-swim motion.

---

## Relationship to performance

Movement quality as defined here **correlates imperfectly** with speed. Sprint specialists may exhibit technique
that differs from distance models. The pack emphasizes **repeatable, load-aware patterns** discussed in open
literature rather than mimicking a single elite swimmer template.

---

## Youth vs adult

Youth:
- Higher stroke rate and shorter stroke length can be normal while learning.
- Fault severity capped for entry/recovery until catch mechanics stabilize (coach override).

Adult:
- Full fault library applies; prior shoulder injury history should trigger conservative language (disclaimer).

Open water:
- Sighting strokes excluded from tier scoring when tagged in metadata.
