# Freestyle Biomechanics — Overview (open literature summary)

This document summarizes **relationships** reported in open-access reviews and kinematic studies of front crawl.
It does not prescribe universal angle targets. Authority: B+ unless noted. Retrieved context: 2026-10-21 pack build.

## Scope and method

Freestyle propulsion arises from **hand/forearm drag and lift** (depending on model), **body orientation**, and
**kick contributions** that vary by distance and skill. Most pool studies use video, underwater cameras, force
paddles, or CFD; CeRebel uses **visible body kinematics** only — findings are proxies, not lab measurements.

Key open-source review themes (representative categories):
- Front crawl kinematics across skill levels (entry, catch, pull, recovery timing).
- Shoulder complex loading and stroke mechanics (clinical reviews — careful language).
- Propulsive vs resistive phases of the arm stroke.
- Role of trunk rotation in reach length and pull depth.

## Kinematic chain in freestyle

Unlike land kinetic chains with ground reaction forces, swimmers create propulsion by **action on the water** while
minimizing drag. A useful coaching chain model:

1. **Body line and roll** set up reach and reduce frontal area.
2. **Entry and extension** place the hand in a position to initiate force without crossing midline excessively.
3. **Catch (EVF)** increases effective surface area oriented to move water backward relative to the swimmer.
4. **Pull and push** accelerate the hand along a path largely under the shoulder; exit near the hip completes the stroke.
5. **Kick** stabilizes hips and adds propulsion especially at sprint rates; at distance, kick amplitude often decreases.

Proximal-to-distal timing appears in literature as **hip/trunk rotation preceding peak hand force**, though exact
phase lags vary by swimmer morphology and stroke rate.

---

## Entry and catch

Studies across skill levels often report differences in:

- **Entry position** relative to shoulder and head.
- **Time from entry to maximum hand velocity** — shorter in elites at same rate in some cohorts.
- **Elbow flexion** developing during early underwater phase.

The coaching construct **early vertical forearm (EVF)** aligns with observations that higher skilled swimmers
orient the forearm to press backward sooner, with the elbow flexed and often visually **above the wrist** in
side view. Exact elbow angles in degrees vary widely; product uses ordinal proxies (elbow above wrist) not
clinical goniometry.

**Dropped elbow** patterns associate with:
- Reduced propulsive impulse in some experimental summaries.
- Increased shoulder abduction/internal rotation moments in modeling papers — interpret as load discussion, not diagnosis.

---

## Pull and push

Mid-pull typically shows **peak hand speed** and substantial trunk rotation toward the pulling side. Pull path
curvature in 3D studies often passes under the swimmer's center of mass projection; wide pulls are associated with
less efficient force orientation in coaching biomechanics texts.

Push phase (finish) contributes non-trivial impulse in some force paddle studies — exiting early may truncate
propulsion. Elbow extension timing interacts with stroke rate: higher rates may shorten push duration while
maintaining impulse via higher acceleration.

---

## Recovery

Recovery style varies:
- **High-elbow recovery** common in distance and technical coaching — reduces swing width and setup for clean entry.
- **Straighter recovery** seen in some sprinters — tradeoffs in shoulder mobility and drag are debated in coaching literature.

Body tracking from side view can classify recovery height qualitatively; front view helps detect crossover setup.

---

## Kick mechanics

Flutter kick:
- Originate from **hip flexion/extension** with relatively **straight knee** at cruise speeds in many texts.
- **Six-beat** kick (six kicks per full arm cycle) common in middle-distance competitive models.
- **Two-beat** kick reduces energy cost in some distance swimmers but demands strong catch/pull.

Kick contributes **stabilization** (hip roll control) even when propulsive fraction is modest. Excessive knee flexion
(bicycle kick) increases drag in beginner cohort descriptions.

---

## Breathing mechanics

Breathing introduces **transient asymmetry**:
- Head rotation reduces need for vertical lift; vertical lift increases drag in pool swimming models.
- Breathing every 2 vs 3 strokes shifts timing asymmetry; bilateral training is a common balance strategy (coaching B).

Biomechanics studies note **torsion** on the spine during breath — usually manageable in healthy swimmers; pain
requires clinical evaluation (disclaimer).

---

## Body roll and drag

Roll amplitude interacts with:
- **Reach length** per stroke (often measured as distance per stroke in pool studies).
- **Shoulder clearance** during recovery.
- **Kick coupling** — flat swimming (low roll) may correlate with shorter reach in learning swimmers.

Over-rotation may correlate with **crossover entry** and snaking hips — product flags co-occurrence patterns.

Drag components (form drag, wave drag) dominate at surface speeds; small postural deviations (head lift, wide legs)
have outsized drag effects in fluid dynamics summaries — qualitative coaching importance, hard to quantify from 2D pose alone.

---

## Skill level and stroke rate / length tradeoff

Elite sprinters vs distance swimmers exhibit different **stroke rate (SPM)** and **stroke length** combinations:
- Sprinters: higher rate, shorter length per cycle sometimes, stronger kick.
- Distance: moderate rate, longer length, economized kick.

Youth swimmers often show **higher stroke rates and shorter lengths** while learning — quality definitions must not
apply elite templates blindly (see Quality/001_quality_definition.txt youth axis).

## Measurement limitations vs research grade

Research studies may use:
- Underwater multi-camera systems
- Force transducers on hand paddles
- Electromyography

CeRebel approximates:
- Wrist/elbow/shoulder trajectories
- Cycle timing from peaks
- Roll from shoulder line tilt

Do not equate app findings with research instrumentation. Confidence flags in Phases/001_phase_model.txt apply.

## Injury and load (non-diagnostic summary)

Open clinical literature discusses **shoulder overuse** in swimmers with multifactorial causes: volume, dry-land
strength, technique patterns, and anatomy. Technique findings such as dropped elbow and overreach are discussed as
**modifiable factors** in some reviews — not sole causes.

Product rules:
- Never diagnose impingement or rotator cuff pathology from video.
- Escalate pain language to disclaimer and human coach/clinician.

---

## Freestyle vs other strokes (glossary pointer)

Backstroke shares roll and catch concepts but supine orientation changes camera requirements.
Breaststroke and butterfly have distinct kick and recovery constraints — not v1 automated findings.
Turns and starts involve wall forces — out of v1 cycle model.
