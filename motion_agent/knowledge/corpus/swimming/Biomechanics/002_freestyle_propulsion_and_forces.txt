# Freestyle — Propulsion, Drag, and Force Proxies (summary)

Authority: B+ open reviews and textbook-level biomechanics summaries. Not for medical or performance guarantees.
English RAG reference for chat grounded in body-visible proxies.

---

## Propulsive vs resistive phases (conceptual)

Swimmers move when **propulsive impulses** exceed **drag and inertial losses**. In arm-dominated models of steady freestyle:

| Phase (coaching) | Typical role in models | Body-visible proxy |
|------------------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Entry / glide | Can be resistive if hand pushes forward too long | Forward hand velocity before catch |
| Catch | Transition; build effective surface area | Elbow flexion, wrist depth |
| Pull | Main propulsive impulse in many studies | Wrist speed peak, trunk rotation |
| Push | Completing impulse; elbow extension | Hand path past hip |
| Recovery | Usually resistive above water | Arm height, width |

Exact force curves require instrumentation; CeRebel uses **speed and path proxies** only.

---

## Hand/forearm orientation (lift and drag debates)

Hydrodynamic models treat the hand/forearm as a **foil** generating lift and drag depending on angle of attack.
Coaching simplification: **press backward on the water** with a vertical or inward-rotated forearm early.

Research notes:
- Angle of attack changes through the pull; no single static angle defines elite swimmers.
- 2D side video **cannot** recover true 3D pitch reliably when submerged.

Product language: "forearm vertical proxy" or "elbow above wrist" instead of stating degrees.

---

## Trunk rotation and impulse

Trunk rotation contributes to:
- Lengthening reach at entry (roll toward recovering side).
- Positioning pulling shoulder for internal rotation and adduction during pull.
- Timing kick beats relative to arm phases.

Kinematic studies report **peak trunk roll** near mid-pull on the pulling side in many cohorts; phase lag varies.
Flat swimming (low roll) may reduce reach and alter pull depth — coaching correlation, individual exceptions exist.

---

## Kick propulsion fraction

Reported kick contributions to total propulsion vary widely by:
- Swimming speed (higher at sprint).
- Kick pattern (2-beat vs 6-beat).
- Skill level and ankle flexibility.

Even when kick propulsion fraction is modest at distance pace, kick ** stabilizes hip position** and supports roll rhythm.
Bicycle kick increases **form drag** in many beginner descriptions — flagged as efficiency fault, not moral judgment.

---

## Stroke rate, stroke length, and speed

Pool performance approximates:

speed ≈ stroke_rate × stroke_length

Body video can estimate **stroke rate** from cycle periods. **Stroke length** needs distance per cycle — requires
pool length markers, known speed, or external timing. Do not invent SWOLF without lap data.

Coaching implication: technique fixes may increase length first for learners; sprinters may prioritize rate and power
after technique baseline.

---

## Asymmetry and breathing side

Breathing every 2 strokes to one side introduces **persistent asymmetry** in roll and pull timing. Many programs
use bilateral breathing in training to balance neuromuscular patterns. Asymmetry findings in CeRebel compare L/R
peaks and periods — not always faults if athlete races with fixed breathing pattern.

---

## Starts, turns, underwater (out of v1)

Streamline off walls and underwater dolphin phases produce large impulses not modeled in arm-cycle v1.
Training docs may reference them; automated phases exclude push-off unless metadata trims glide.

---

## Open water differences

Waves, sighting, and wetsuit buoyancy alter:
- Head lift frequency (sighting strokes).
- Roll amplitude (stability in chop).
- Kick duty cycle (navigation and drafting).

Apply open_water confidence flags before strict head-line faults.

---

## Youth biomechanics notes

Children and adolescents differ in:
- Limb segment ratios affecting reach and pull radius.
- Motor learning stage — catch may appear late while kick-dominant swimming persists.
- Training volume tolerance — see Training/002_youth_development.txt.

Avoid adult elite templates for youth tier scoring.

## Practical RAG answer template

When users ask "why is my pull weak?" without force data:

1. Ask/clarify camera angle and cycle count (if chat context allows).
2. Reference phase model: entry → catch EVF proxy → pull peak → push finish.
3. Link visible faults (dropped elbow, short push, wide path) to **coaching cues**, not Newtons of force.
4. Note that **pull buoy**, paddles, and bands change mechanics — outside plain stroke video.
5. Include disclaimer for shoulder pain.
