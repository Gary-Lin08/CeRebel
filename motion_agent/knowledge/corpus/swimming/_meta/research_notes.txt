segment only (Training/), not elite freestyle biomechanics findings.
  URL: https://www.worldaquatics.com/development/programmes/aquatics-community/

3. PUBLIC COACHING — USA SWIMMING (B)
================================================================================

USA Swimming University: Core Certification, ADM tracks, Legacy Project, Summer League Coaching
(90 min: quality coaching, stroke fundamentals, kids/parents, planning). Member vs non-member
pricing; many stroke mini-courses (e.g. "Key Points of Freestyle" for 10&U) require login.
  URL: https://www.usaswimming.org/coaches-leaders/coaches/education

American Development Model — freestyle progression themes (summarized):
  - Streamlined push-off, breakout kick-to-pull, bilateral breathing comfort, legal flip turn.
  - Named drill families: timing the breath, one-eye breathing, early vertical forearm (EVF),
    one-arm drills, 6-count switch, vertical sculling.
  - Levels tie stroke count, pace clock use, and legal 100s/IM by level — separate youth norms.
  URLs: https://www.usaswimming.org/coaches-leaders/coaches/american-development-model
        https://www.usaswimming.org/docs/default-source/coaching-resourcesdocuments/adm-competence-progressions.pdf

Regional Coach Clinics (2026): "national-level readiness" holistic framework — not a single
technique doctrine; useful for long-term athlete development copy, not phase-detector events.
  URL: https://www.usaswimming.org/coaches-leaders/connect-learn/programs-services/clinics-workshops/regional-coach-clinics

4. FREESTYLE / FRONT CRAWL BIOMECHANICS — PMC & OPEN REVIEWS (B+ / A)
================================================================================

4A. Propulsion, drag, and within-cycle velocity (B+, CC BY where noted)

Active drag narrative review (Lopes et al. 2022, PMC9630912, CC BY 4.0):
  Synthesizes experimental/numerical active drag methods; technique and body position affect
  drag–propulsion balance. Product: frame faults as "increased drag risk / efficiency" not
  absolute speed promises.

Intracycle velocity variation scoping review (Fernandes et al. 2023, PMC10044880, CC BY 4.0):
  Front crawl dominates literature; IVV quantification methods diverge; links to coordination,
  fatigue, stroke rate/length tradeoffs. High heterogeneity — avoid single IVV target.

4B. Stroke coordination and trunk roll (B+)

400-m front-crawl meta-analysis (Correia et al. 2023, PMC9909090, CC BY 4.0):
  Pooled index of coordination (IdC) ≈ −11% → catch-up coordination pattern in aggregated
  400-m trials; arm-stroke efficiency ηp ≈ 35%; mean speed ~1.34 m/s in included studies
  (below world-record pace). Heterogeneity >75% — population references only.

400-m pace coordination stability (Schnitzler et al. 2009, PMC3761493):
  During all-out 400-m, stroke length and speed drop with fatigue while IdC and stroke rate
  can remain stable — coordination metrics may persist as SL falls. Useful for interpreting
  late-race visible shortening without over-cuing SR alone.

Elite trunk roll strategies (Niu et al. 2026, PMC13443057):
  Three upper-trunk vs pelvic roll strategies (upper-trunk-leading, pelvic-leading, near-synchronous);
  near-synchronous most frequent (~53%); faster within-athlete speed increases odds of
  upper-trunk-leading. Conclusion: individualized coordination, not one optimal roll phase lock.
  License: verify on PMC page before extended quotation.

Coaching alignment (USA ADM): shoulder/hip roll and EVF cues are pedagogical — reconcile with
research showing inter-individual trunk strategies and speed-dependent strategy shifts.

4C. Training interventions (B+, JSSM)

S&C meta-analysis (Kwok et al. 2021, PMC8488830):
  Resistance/core training can improve sprint front crawl and stroke biomechanics; resistance
  may increase stroke rate; plyometrics help starts; turn evidence limited. Findings belong in
  Training/ — not automatic fault labels from body pose alone.

4D. Underwater undulatory kick — starts/turns (B+)

UUS systematic review (West et al. 2022, PMC9334501, Sports Medicine - Open):
  Foot acceleration, kick frequency, hip/knee/ankle angular velocity and ROM correlate with
  underwater velocity; prone vs supine differences; ~15 m maximum underwater distance (rule context).
  Body-first above-water pack may only weakly observe dolphin kick; flag start/turn phases when
  camera sees breakout.

4E. EMG and shoulder load (B+ / A)

Front-crawl sEMG review (Kwok et al. 2023, PMC9982531):
  Literature biased to upper-limb muscles; trunk/legs and start/turn phases under-represented.
  Supports whole-body cues (rotation, kick) rather than arm-only fault cards.

Swimmer's shoulder muscle synergies (Matsuura et al. 2023, PMC10732115, Sports Health):
  Elite swimmers with shoulder pain history show different muscle coordination during recovery
  (front crawl and backstroke): reduced upper-limb contribution when trunk rolls. Clinical level 4
  case-control — product may mention recovery-phase trunk–arm timing as load awareness, NOT
  diagnosis or "you have swimmer's shoulder."

5. PHASE MODEL SEED (BODY-FIRST, PROVISIONAL FOR brain_plan / Phases/)
================================================================================

Suggested 6–8 phases for side-on pool video (above water, one stroke side visible):

  1. ENTRY / EXTENSION — hand enters, arm extends forward
  2. CATCH / EVF — elbow flexion increases, forearm vertical tendency (proxy only)
  3. PULL / PROPULSION — hand path under body, peak shoulder/elbow flexion
  4. PUSH / EXIT — hand accelerates backward, release toward hip
  5. RECOVERY — elbow exits, arm swings forward (high shoulder load visibility)
  6. BREATH (optional tagged sub-phase) — head rotation when bilateral breathing visible

Underwater push-off / breakout: separate tags if feet and wall visible; else omit.

Cross-cycle metrics: stroke rate proxy (cadence of shoulder peaks), stroke length proxy
(shoulder–hip displacement per cycle if scale unknown — trend only), trunk roll amplitude
(shoulder vs hip rotation differential).

| Theme | Phase | Visible signal (monocular side) | Why it matters (performance framing) | Cue/drill pointer (B sources) | Sources |
|-------|-------|----------------------------------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------|---------|
| Flat swim / low roll | Pull–recovery | Shoulders stay square; hip roll << shoulder roll | Associated with shorter propulsive window in coaching literature; trunk reviews show roll strategies vary | 6-count switch; emphasize hip-led rotation | ADM; PMC13443057 |
| Cross-over entry | Entry | Hand crosses midline in front of head | Increases drag / snaking in coaching tradition; drag review links alignment to active drag | Enter wide; "track parallel" | ADM EVF progressions; PMC9630912 |
| Weak EVF / dropped elbow (proxy) | Catch–pull | Elbow stays high; wrist leads before vertical forearm | Reduces propulsive surface in coaching models | EVF drills, sculling | USA ADM |
| Over-rotation on breath | Recovery | Long pause with head up; hip drops | Disrupts rhythm; IVV/coordination literature links rhythm to velocity fluctuations | One-eye breathing; timing breath | ADM; PMC10044880 |
| Asymmetric stroke | Full cycle | Left/right shoulder peak timing or arc mismatch | Bilateral breathing and injury awareness themes in EMG/shoulder studies | One-arm drills both sides | PMC9982531; PMC10732115 |
| Shortened stroke late race | Pull | Same SR proxy but smaller reach / elbow extension | Matches fatigue-induced SL drop with stable coordination (400-m study) | Hold SL cues; aerobic context | PMC3761493 |

No single numeric elbow angle or roll degree should be marketed as "correct" for all athletes.

================================================================================
7. BODY TRACKING FEASIBILITY (B, INTERNAL)
================================================================================

Good side-camera visibility: shoulder/elbow/wrist timing, head turn for breath, hip roll proxy,
kick knee flexion rhythm (if legs not turbid), stroke cadence.

Poor or absent: exact hand pitch, pitch of forearm in highly turbid water, precise catch angle,
underwater pull path, wall distance for 15 m breakout, finger pitch.

OpenSim: shoulder complex highly constrained in water; use trends and phase timing, not
lab-grade joint moments.
