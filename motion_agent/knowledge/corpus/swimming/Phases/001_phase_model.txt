# Freestyle (Front Crawl) — Phase Model (body-first, side-view friendly)

## Overview

Eight phases describe one freestyle stroke cycle on the **pulling arm**, with parallel kick and body-roll signals
across the cycle. Phases overlap by design: breathing occurs during recovery and early catch on the breathing side;
kick continues throughout.

Default camera: **side profile**, swimmer moving left-to-right in frame (adjust mirroring in software).
Authority: ProductLogic + summarized open biomechanics (B+); see Biomechanics/001_overview.txt.

Stroke side metadata: `stroke_side=right` means the right arm is the pulling arm for event anchoring in this document.
Mirror joint names for left-arm analysis.

---

## Phase 1 — Recovery (swim_phase_recovery)

order_index: 1
start_event: E_EXIT (previous cycle) or clip start if mid-cycle
end_event: E_ENTRY

Description:
Pulling arm travels forward above or through the water surface with elbow higher than wrist (high-elbow recovery
is typical in distance freestyle; straighter recovery appears in some sprint models). Shoulders roll so the
recovering arm clears the water without excessive lateral sway.

Body-measurable signals:
- Pulling wrist above shoulder line proxy (2D side view) during most of recovery.
- Elbow flexion moderate (roughly 90–130° visual band — not a medical prescription).
- Contralateral arm underwater in pull or catch unless double-arm drill.

Typical duration: 0.25–0.55 s at moderate pace (cycle period ~1.0–1.8 s).

Detector notes:
- E_EXIT: pulling wrist vertical velocity turns positive toward surface; forward speed drops vs pull peak.
- If recovery is submerged “doggy paddle,” wrist may stay low — flag low confidence for recovery metrics.

Fault window: fault_low_elbow_recovery, fault_crossover_entry_setup (late recovery path).

## Phase 2 — Entry and extension (swim_phase_entry)

order_index: 2
start_event: E_ENTRY
end_event: E_CATCH

Description:
Hand enters water ahead of the shoulder with fingers first (ideal) and arm extends forward along the body line.
Minimal splash and **no crossing the centerline** at entry are coaching targets; underwater extension sets up catch.

Body-measurable signals:
- Pulling wrist reaches forward local maximum before downward motion.
- Shoulder elevation relative to trunk stable — shrugging through entry flags fault_shoulder_hike_entry.
- Head neutral unless breathing; eyes down in pool swimming.

Events:
- E_ENTRY: wrist forward position peak + downward velocity onset; often synchronized with opposite arm pull peak.

Typical duration: 0.08–0.20 s.

Fault window: fault_overreach_entry, fault_crossover_entry, fault_flat_hand_entry (visual splash proxy only).

---

## Phase 3 — Catch and early vertical forearm (swim_phase_catch)

order_index: 3
start_event: E_CATCH
end_event: E_PULL_MID (internal; between catch and pull peak)

Description:
After entry, the forearm and hand orient to press backward on the water — **early vertical forearm (EVF)** in coaching
language. Elbow flexion increases while upper arm internally rotates; wrist depth proxy increases in side view.

Body-measurable signals:
- Elbow flexion rate positive; elbow stays **higher** than wrist in side view during successful catch (EVF proxy).
- Minimal downward “pressing” before backward motion — fault dropped_elbow_catch if wrist leads elbow downward.

Events:
- E_CATCH: elbow flexion acceleration peak after entry; optional delay 0.05–0.15 s after E_ENTRY at steady pace.

Typical duration: 0.10–0.25 s.

Fault window: fault_dropped_elbow, fault_sculling_outside, fault_pause_at_catch.

---

## Phase 4 — Pull (propulsive phase) (swim_phase_pull)

order_index: 4
start_event: E_PULL_MID
end_event: E_PUSH_START

Description:
Hand and forearm accelerate backward under the body; trunk rotates toward pulling side. Propulsion is primarily
from arm action in many steady-state freestyle models, with kick maintaining alignment.

Body-measurable signals:
- Pulling wrist speed reaches cycle maximum (E_PULL_PEAK).
- Elbow flexion near maximum mid-pull then begins extending into push.
- Hip and shoulder roll toward pulling side — phase coupled with swim_phase_body_roll.

Events:
- E_PULL_PEAK: argmax pulling-wrist speed between E_CATCH and E_EXIT.

Typical duration: 0.15–0.35 s.

Fault window: fault_wide_pull, fault_pull_under_body, fault_asymmetric_pull_timing.

---

## Phase 5 — Push and hand exit (swim_phase_push)

order_index: 5
start_event: E_PUSH_START
end_event: E_EXIT

Description:
Hand accelerates backward past the hip; elbow extends; hand exits near hip or upper thigh. Incomplete push —
exiting too early — reduces propulsive length.

Body-measurable signals:
- Wrist trajectory passes hip keypoint proxy before E_EXIT.
- Elbow extension rate peak near exit.
- Exit with hand close to body vs wide — fault wide_exit.

Events:
- E_PUSH_START: elbow extension rate turns positive after pull peak (heuristic).
- E_EXIT: wrist breaks surface or lateral speed drops entering recovery.

Typical duration: 0.08–0.18 s.

Fault window: fault_short_push, fault_wide_exit, fault_upward_exit.

---

## Phase 6 — Breathing (swim_phase_breathe)

order_index: 6 (parallel overlay)
start_event: E_BREATHE_START
end_event: E_BREATHE_END

Description:
On breathing strokes, head rotates to the side with mouth clearing water; one goggle stays in when possible.
Breathing usually occurs during recovery of the opposite arm or early catch of pulling arm depending on pattern.

Body-measurable signals:
- Head yaw toward breathing side peaks at E_BREATHE.
- Lateral head displacement relative to spine — excessive lift flags fault_excessive_head_lift.
- Timing: breath should not coincide with maximum pull force on breathing side (fault late_breath_timing).

Events:
- E_BREATHE: head yaw local maximum; E_BREATHE_END: head returns within neutral band.

Typical duration: 0.20–0.45 s for inhale window.

Fault window: fault_excessive_head_lift, fault_bilateral_imbalance_breath, fault_hold_breath_tension.

---

## Phase 7 — Body roll (swim_phase_body_roll)

order_index: 7 (continuous)
start_event: continuous with cycle
end_event: continuous

Description:
Freestyle uses trunk rotation to lengthen reach and reduce shoulder impingement risk in many coaching models.
Roll amplitude varies by distance vs sprint; open water often shows higher roll with sighting inserts.

Body-measurable signals:
- Shoulder line tilt oscillates with stroke period.
- Hips rotate with shoulders — fault flat_swim if roll amplitude below athlete baseline.
- Over-roll can correlate with crossover entry — fault_over_rotation.

Measurable proxy: peak-to-peak shoulder tilt angle from horizontal per cycle.

Typical roll peak: often 30–45° visual band for many trained swimmers in side view — individual variation large.

---

## Phase 8 — Kick (swim_phase_kick)

order_index: 8 (continuous)
start_event: continuous
end_event: continuous

Description:
Flutter kick from hips with relatively straight knees at surface swimming speeds; ankle plantar flexion at kick
end. Two-beat, four-beat, and six-beat patterns change timing relative to arm cycle.

Body-measurable signals:
- Ankle vertical velocity oscillation at 2× or 3× stroke frequency (pattern dependent).
- Knee flexion modest — excessive knee bend flags fault_bicycle_kick.
- Kick depth — feet breaking surface may indicate fault_surface_kick_splash (efficiency context).

Detector notes:
- E_KICK_PEAK: ankle speed peak; align to contralateral or ipsilateral arm per detected pattern (low confidence).

---

## Cycle assembly and multi-stroke sessions

- Segment cycles using alternating E_ENTRY events on each wrist.
- Report **left/right asymmetry** when at least 3 cycles per side are detected.
- Exclude push-off glide: first cycle after wall contact often has suppressed kick — mark metadata.

## Event summary table

| Event ID | Description | Primary proxy |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| E_ENTRY | Hand enters water | Wrist forward peak → down |
| E_CATCH | Catch initiation | Elbow flexion accel post-entry |
| E_PULL_PEAK | Max pull speed | Max wrist speed underwater |
| E_PUSH_START | Push phase start | Elbow extension accel |
| E_EXIT | Hand exits | Wrist surface break / speed drop |
| E_BREATHE | Breath peak | Head yaw max |
| E_KICK_PEAK | Kick peak | Ankle vertical speed max |

## Confidence flags

- camera_front_only: reduce catch and elbow findings confidence.
- open_water_sighting: suppress head-lift fault severity unless pool baseline known.
- low_fps: events under 30 fps widen timing uncertainty ±1 frame.
