1. CONSENSUS SPIKE PHASE MODELS (RECONCILE IN Phases/001)
================================================================================

1A. Upper-limb / clinical five-phase model (serve and spike shared pattern)
  Source: Reeser & Fleisig lineage; Reeser et al. Sports Health 2010 (PMC3445065).
  Phases: approach → takeoff → arm cocking → arm acceleration → follow-through.
  Boundary: max shoulder external rotation separates cocking from acceleration.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3445065/
  Authority A (clinical) | License: © 2010 The Author(s); Sage (PMC free access, not CC-BY).

1B. Whole-spike performance phases (lab, 3D mocap)
  Source: Serrien et al. 2016 — approach, cocking, acceleration (VICON 3D; SPM).
  Top-level vs junior elite differ in impact speed and jump height; pelvis/trunk orientation
  angles showed invariant patterns while angular velocities differ by expertise and sex.
  URL: https://doi.org/10.13189/saj.2016.040403
  Authority B+ | License: verify on SAJ/Horizon open-access record (often CC BY for SAJ).

1C. Jump–airborne–landing macro phases (training intervention studies)
  Source: Kim et al. 2024/2025 Sci Rep spike training RCT (PMC12804966).
  Jumping phase (approach + takeoff), airborne phase (COM apex), landing phase (deceleration).
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12804966/
  Authority B+ | License: Scientific Reports — typically CC BY 4.0 (confirm PMC record).

1D. FIVB coaching progression (qualitative, technique teaching)
  Source: FIVB Coaches Manual Level I — Chapter VI Teaching Basic Individual Techniques;
  approach run, jump, hit, landing; mini/mass volleyball scaling in Chapter V.
  URL: https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/FIVB_Coach_Manual_EN.pdf
  Authority B | License: © FIVB; summarize pedagogy only.

2. APPROACH & SPIKE-JUMP BIOMECHANICS (ADULT LAB — NOT PRESCRIPTIVE TARGETS)
================================================================================

RSJ-1L vs RSJ-2L (three-step approach spike jump, male college, force plates + mocap):
  Source: Tai et al. Appl. Sci. 2021 (MDPI), doi 10.3390/app11062710.
  RSJ-1L: faster approach velocity, higher peak vertical GRF (~3.0 vs ~1.6 BW on plant leg),
  greater ankle/knee/hip moments, shorter push-off time, less jump height, stiffer knee at contact.
  RSJ-2L: more countermovement, higher jump height, longer push-off.
  Tactical mapping (authors): RSJ-1L ↔ quick spike / short set; RSJ-2L ↔ high set / more CM jump.
  Authority B+ | License: CC BY 4.0 (MDPI Applied Sciences).

Approach step count (2 vs 3 steps, female elite):
  Source: Topics in Exercise Science and Kinesiology 2023, doi 10.9741/2766-2187.1074.
  3-step with higher horizontal velocity: more dominant-knee flexion to brake horizontal momentum;
  faster hip/knee/ankle extension on non-dominant leg at takeoff.
  Authority B+ | Open-access journal article (verify license on publisher page).

Spike jump height correlates (experienced players, 3D):
  Source: Wagner et al. lineage; IJSM spike jump study (doi 10.1055/s-0029-1224177).
  Significant correlations: max horizontal COM velocity, CMJ/SJ height, right knee flexion–extension
  ROM, left shoulder hyperextension angular velocity; left vs right foot distance to COM asymmetry.
  Authority B+ | License: Thieme — abstract/subscriber; cite relationships only.

Classic 3D spike (international males):
  Source: Coleman et al. 1993 J Sports Sci (doi 10.1080/02640419308729999).
  Mean jump height ~0.62 m; COM vertical velocity at takeoff ~3.59 m/s; ball speed ~27 m/s
  correlated with max humerus angular velocity (r ≈ 0.75).
  Authority B+ | License: Taylor & Francis — summarize published abstract statistics only.

Systematic review — spike kinematics (18 studies, 3D):
  Source: Oliveira et al. Int J Sports Sci Coach 2020, doi 10.1177/1747954119899881.
  Themes: jump height ↔ COM velocity; ball speed ↔ hitting-arm and trunk velocity variables;
  cross-court from pos. 4 ↔ trunk rotation; sex differences; injury links inconclusive.
  Quality mix: high/good/limited studies — do not pool into single angle norms.
  Authority B+ | License: Sage — restricted access; use abstract + cited relationships.

Successful vs faulty spikes (elite juniors, game-like):
  Source: Sarvestan et al. Sensors 2021 (PMC7917820), doi 10.3390/s21041345.
  ROM patterning similar between outcomes; upper-limb angular velocity differs 35–45% and 76–100%
  of spike duration — coaches should emphasize arm speed/coordination, not only jump height.
  Authority B+ | License: CC BY 4.0 (MDPI Sensors).

3. UPPER LIMB AT CONTACT & SHOULDER LOAD (CAREFUL WORDING)
================================================================================

Spike/serve upper limb at contact (lab cohort):
  Source: PMC3445065.
  Hard spike: shoulder abducted/externally rotated in cocking; acceleration = internal rotation +
  elbow extension; contact with flexed/ internally rotated shoulder, extended elbow.
  Roll shot / float serve: lower IR velocity, elbow extension velocity, ball speed.
  Authority A | License: Sage via PMC — summarize only.

Ball–hand contact force effect on modeled shoulder torque:
  Source: Howard et al. J Sports Sci Med 2023 (PMC10499142), doi 10.52082/jssm.2023.488.
  Adolescent females; estimated hand force ~416 ± 99 N (range ~238–672 N); including ball contact
  force flips average modeled internal shoulder rotation torque sign/magnitude vs models that omit it.
  Product: shoulder load awareness from visible arm speed + repeated volume — not torque diagnosis.
  Authority B+ | License: JSSM open access (check PMC "Copyright and License" — typically author/journal OA terms).

4. LANDING AFTER SPIKE / BLOCK (INJURY-AWARENESS, NOT DIAGNOSIS)
================================================================================

Systematic review — spike and block landings (n=14 studies):
  Source: Kurt Tuç & Cengizel, Turk J Kinesiol 2026, doi 10.31459/turkjkin.1838171.
  Key variables: valgus–varus, joint angles/moments, ROM, GRF, stiffness, strength.
  Female players: greater ROM and knee valgus ↔ higher reported injury risk in literature reviewed.
  Spike landings: forward attack → horizontal + vertical momentum dissipation.
  Authority B+ | License: OPEN ACCESS journal PDF (DergiPark); confirm reuse terms on download.

Four-week spike-specific training (jump + landing kinetics):
  Source: PMC12804966.
  Post-training: higher jump COM, altered knee flexion moment during jump phase, reduced knee
  extension moment / vertical joint force on landing (performance + landing-load themes).
  Authority B+ | License: CC BY 4.0 (typical Sci Rep — verify).

Epidemiology context (cited in training studies): high seasonal spike volumes and landing impacts
up to ~7× body weight on single-leg landings in literature — use for workload disclaimers, not
clip-level inference.

  - E_JUMP_PEAK ≈ pelvis/COM vertical maximum between plant and landing.

  surface=beach in session metadata.
