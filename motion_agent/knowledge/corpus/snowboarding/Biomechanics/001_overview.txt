# Snowboarding Biomechanics — Overview (open-literature summary)
# Authority: B+ where cited as reviews/kinematics; not prescriptive medicine
# Product: body-first pose/OpenSim; board forces estimated unless instrumented

================================================================================
SCOPE
================================================================================

This document summarizes biomechanical relationships useful for CeRebel snowboarding coaching:
linked turning on snow, basic jump takeoff and landing, and aerial rotation measurement conventions.
It does not replace instrumented studies with on-snow force plates or binding load cells.

Cross-reference product files:
- snowboard_flip_conventions.txt — frontflip/backflip and intrinsic somersault
- snowboard_aerial_vocab.txt — frontside/backside vs flip axis language

================================================================================
COORDINATE FRAMES AND MEASUREMENT
================================================================================

World frame (OpenSim ground, Y-up):
- COM trajectory, horizontal speed, and vertical velocity for takeoff/landing events.
- root_euler_continuous_deg and root_cumulative_rotation_deg for total rotation extent in flips and spins.

Rider-intrinsic frame:
- intrinsic_somersault_* for somersault direction (positive ≈ backflip direction per product convention).
- Do not classify front vs back flip from world Euler X sign alone when the rider reorients in air.

Pose-only limitations:
- "Edge angle" and true board normal are not observed without board model or dual-camera calibration.
- Shoulder–pelvis lateral offset proxies angulation, not edge degrees.
- Estimated GRF from inverse dynamics are model-dependent, especially during impact and off-axis landings.

================================================================================
STANCE AND SEGMENT COUPLING
================================================================================

Regular and goofy stances reverse lead/trail limb roles but not basic joint coupling:
- Turn initiation commonly involves lead-limb flexion and COM path curvature in the frontal plane (relative to fall line).
- Upper-body counter-rotation can decouple shoulder line from pelvis line to manage balance and view; advanced riders use
  larger counter-rotation amplitudes than beginners (skill-dependent, B coaching observation).

Ankle–knee–hip chain:
- Athletic flexion distributes load along the chain; locked knees reduce available range for absorption and unweighting
  (general lower-limb mechanics, B+).
- Boot stiffness constrains ankle motion — visible pose may under-estimate ankle contribution.

================================================================================
TURNING MECHANICS (ALPINE / FREERIDE BASICS)
================================================================================

Center of mass path:
- In linked turns, COM traces a sinusoidal or curved path across the slope; turn radius relates to speed and edge control
  (conceptual mechanics, B+ alpine sport science summaries).

Pressure and edge control (conceptual — board not tracked):
- Riders modulate pressure along board length through fore-aft weight shift and lateral angulation.
- Skidded turns dissipate energy via friction; carved turns (when achieved) bend turn shape with cleaner COM path — product
  flags upright skid proxies, not carve certification.

Rotational dynamics:
- Yaw rotation about vertical axis changes heading; roll (bank) relates to inclination into turn.
- Excessive early upper-body yaw can precede skidding when lower body has not established edge engagement (coaching
  kinematics, B).

Estimated loading:
- Centripetal acceleration scales with speed²/radius; faster tight turns increase lateral demand on lower limbs in
  simplified models (physics B+). Do not quote numeric G without validated speed and radius.

================================================================================
JUMP TAKEOFF (AIR FAMILY)
================================================================================

Countermovement pattern:
- Preload (eccentric knee/hip flexion) followed by concentric extension resembles countermovement jumps on firm surfaces
  (extensive jump literature, B+). Snow adds compliance and binding/bottom interaction not captured in lab CMJ alone.

Timing:
- Extension should peak near takeoff event proxy (max COM vertical velocity). Late or incomplete extension reduces COM
  velocity at lip contact (training observations, B+ analogies).

Arm role:
- Arms contribute to angular momentum in spins; for straight airs, asymmetric arm motion can induce unwanted rotation
  (B).

================================================================================
LANDING MECHANICS
================================================================================

Impact absorption:
- Successful landings show rapid knee and hip flexion after contact, increasing time over which COM decelerates
  (impulse–momentum relationship, B+).
- Stiff-legged landings shorten absorption time and increase peak estimated forces in simulation (B+ — model sensitive).

Symmetry:
- Bilateral flexion sharing load reduces single-limb demand in models; one-foot dominance increases estimated knee moments
  on loaded side (B+ caution, not clinical diagnosis).

Back-seat posture:
- COM posterior to ankle proxy increases quadriceps demand and reduces forward control in simplified models (B coaching +
  B+ squat/landing mechanics analogies).

Snow compliance:
- Soft snow increases penetration and contact duration; ice reduces compliance — same pose fault may produce different
  estimated loads; product should not compare landings across conditions without metadata.

================================================================================
AERIAL ROTATION AND OFF-AXIS TRICKS
================================================================================

Multi-axis rotation:
- Cork and off-axis tricks combine somersault and twist components; single-axis knee/hip metrics insufficient for trick
  naming (product uses intrinsic tools + RGB).

Grab phase:
- Trunk flexion for indy/mute/etc. changes inertia distribution; not classified from pose alone without RGB.

================================================================================
FATIGUE AND SESSION LOAD
================================================================================

Repeated jumps and hard landings accumulate mechanical load (B+ training science). Pose-based products can count
landing stiffness faults per session as a **coaching load awareness** metric, not a medical training load score.

Youth:
- Growth plates and skill acquisition prioritize volume control on jumps (NGO guidance, A/B) — separate youth severity in
  Quality checkpoints.

================================================================================
RELATION TO FIS COMPETITION BIOMECHANICS
================================================================================

Judged events (halfpipe, slopestyle, big air) score amplitude, difficulty, and execution — not directly inferable from
body pose without official judging inputs. SBX and alpine racing add gate timing and line constraints (see fis/ RAG).

Equipment rules (helmets, bindings) are A+ federation content — retrieve from fis/, do not restate numeric homologation
here.
