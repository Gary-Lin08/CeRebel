# Snowboarding Sport Pack — Disclaimer and Safe Use

## Not medical advice

CeRebel snowboarding content summarizes **open coaching literature, biomechanics reviews, instructor
progressions, and federation safety expectations**. It is **not** a substitute for licensed medical care,
physical therapy diagnosis, or individualized return-to-sport protocols after concussion, ACL/MCL injury,
wrist fracture, or spinal trauma.

- Head impacts, loss of consciousness, neck pain, or persistent dizziness after a fall require immediate
  medical evaluation — do not rely on an app finding to resume riding.
- Knee pain, swelling, or giving-way during turns or landings warrants in-person assessment.
- Youth and adolescent riders have distinct growth, strength, and cognitive risk profiles; follow resort,
  school, and national governing body age rules for terrain access and competition.

## Not avalanche, mountain safety, or patrol authority

This pack does not replace avalanche education, backcountry travel protocols, or ski patrol instructions.
Terrain choice, binding DIN settings, helmet use, and course inspection remain the responsibility of the rider,
coach, and venue officials.

## Not a judging or scoring system

FIS and WSPL judging criteria, degree-of-difficulty tables, and race results are **not** computed from body
pose alone. For official wording, use retrieved passages from `knowledge/corpus/fis/` — do not invent rule text.

## Body tracking limitations on snow

Single-camera 2D pose estimation can mis-estimate:

- Knee and hip flexion when the rider is foreshortened up-slope or down-slope.
- Trunk lean and “edge angle” proxies when the board is edge-on to the camera.
- Arm position during grabs and spins when wrists overlap the torso or are occluded by jacket bulk.
- Landing knee valgus when snow spray, other riders, or low frame rate blur the lower body.

Findings describe **visible tendencies** with stated confidence — not laboratory force-plate truth. OpenSim
inverse dynamics produce **estimated** ground reaction forces; treat them as model-dependent, especially during
hard landings and off-axis rotations.

## Aerial rotation and flip language

Frontflip vs backflip and spin direction must follow product conventions in `snowboard_flip_conventions.txt`
and `snowboard_aerial_vocab.txt`. World-frame Euler angles alone are insufficient for trick naming in 360°
and off-axis (cork) maneuvers.

## No guaranteed performance outcomes

Equipment tuning, snow conditions, fatigue, fear, and practice volume dominate progression. Automated cues may
support movement patterns discussed in coaching literature but **do not promise** faster times, higher scores,
or injury prevention for any individual.

## Biomechanical “norms”

Published studies report **ranges across skill levels, stances, and terrain**. Stance width, binding angles, and
board stiffness change appearance. This pack avoids arbitrary degree targets as universal rules, especially for youth.

## Landing load

Repeated stiff-legged landings or large extension landings can increase stress on knees and spine (clinical and
biomechanical consensus). Automated landing findings are **coaching hints**, not clinical diagnosis.

## Coaching relationship

Automated briefs support certified instructors, coaches, and riders who already have a lesson plan. They do not
replace on-snow supervision, beginner lift etiquette training, or professional instruction for first-day riders.

## Emergency

For acute injury on hill, follow resort emergency procedures and seek immediate medical attention when needed.

## Product behavior

The motion agent may run in `snowboarding` prompt mode with RAG over this pack plus FIS corpora. Retrieved text
does not override this disclaimer or local law. Users remain responsible for decisions on snow.
