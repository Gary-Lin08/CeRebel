1. PRODUCT PHASE MODEL (BODY-ONLY — RECONCILE WITH brain_plan.txt)
================================================================================

CeRebel v1 kick families: instep drive (laces power) | side-foot pass (inside foot push).

Shared phase chain (UI IDs): approach → plant_prep → plant → backswing → forward_swing →
contact_proxy → follow.

contact_proxy = argmax ||v(kicking_ankle)|| in analysis window; NOT verified foot–ball contact.

Research-backed phase language for Biomechanics/001 (map to above IDs):

| Research label (literature) | Typical body events | Maps to CeRebel phase |
|-----------------------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| Preparation / run-up      | CoM toward ball; final stride length | approach, plant_prep |
| Support-foot plant        | Lead leg decel; bracing | plant |
| Backswing / leg cocking   | Hip ext + knee flex on kicking leg | backswing |
| Forward swing / acceleration | Hip/knee extension; foot speed rise | forward_swing |
| Impact / ball contact     | Peak foot speed; foot-ball interaction | contact_proxy (proxy only) |
| Follow-through            | Decel; recovery step; balance | follow |

Four-phase technical model (Aedo-Muñoz et al. systematic review, 2017–2020 literature):
  preparation → roll (backswing) → impact → follow-up.
  URL: https://www.revistas.una.ac.cr/index.php/mhsalud/article/view/12638
  Authority: B+ (open journal SR; kinematic/kinetic variable tables — use as taxonomy, not single norms).

2. INSTEP KICK BIOMECHANICS (PRIMARY FOR FINDINGS — B+ / A WHERE NOTED)
================================================================================

2A. Proximal-to-distal sequencing (consensus narrative review)
  Source: Kellis E, Katis A. J Sports Sci Med. 2007;6(2):154-65. PMC3786235.
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3786235/
  Authority: B+ (highly cited review; lab-heavy literature)
  Key points for body-first coaching:
    - Multi-joint kick: segmental rotations in multiple planes.
    - Angular velocity peaks in order: **thigh → shank → foot** (proximal-to-distal).
    - Backswing: thigh decelerates (motion-dependent moment from shank + hip muscles).
    - Forward swing: knee extensor moment + thigh–shank interaction accelerates shank.
    - **Foot-ball contact quality** drives final ball speed, path, spin (not visible in v1 product).
    - Max instep ball speed literature range ~**18–35 m/s** depending on skill, age, approach, dominance.
    - **Accuracy** instructions → lower joint/segment velocities vs max-power kicks.
    - Gap: most studies = max power under lab; need more “fast but accurate” work for passing copy.

2B. Full-body / tension arc / whip (coaching metaphor grounded in review)
  Source: Shan G, Zhang X. BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil. 2011;3:23. Open access.
  URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1758-2555-3-23
  DOI: 10.1186/1758-2555-3-23
  Authority: B+
  Key points:
    - Effective instep kick uses **whole body**; “tension arc” + **whip-like** leg release.
    - Trunk flexion, run-up angle, post-contact posture differ with training, sex, skill.
    - Adductor magnus repetitive strain noted in training — load/volume caution (not diagnosis).
    - Research–practice gap: lab methods not yet “user-friendly” for daily coaching — fits CeRebel
      body-video value proposition if uncertainty is disclosed.

2C. Comprehensive kick review (support leg, trunk, footwear — cite summarized)
  Source: Lees A. J Sports Sci. 2010;28(8):805-17.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2010.481305
  Authority: B+ (paywalled; use abstract + known themes in summaries)
  Themes for product (no ball tracking):
    - Technique spans **approach through ball flight**; upper body, support leg, pelvis influence
      kicking leg mechanics and foot-ball impact.
    - Product scope stops at body follow-through; do not claim launch/spin findings.

2D. Sex-based differences (meta-analysis — separate youth/adult copy)
  Source: “It’s not all about power” SR/MA, Sports Biomechanics 2021.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2021.1981426
  Authority: B+ (23 studies, 455 soccer players)
  Key points:
    - Males: higher ball velocity; higher ankle/foot/toe linear speeds; greater ankle plantarflexion
      at strike; higher hip/knee torques and ball-to-foot velocity ratios (lab cohorts).
    - Females: larger **trunk flexion range** than males in compared studies.
    - **Skill level within sex** may matter more than sex alone for coaching cues.
    - Product: avoid single universal angle targets; offer separate bands where evidence exists.

2E. Skilled vs novice (coaching)
  Skilled players: “tension arc” power generation; novices differ (cited in 2021 SR above).
  Use qualitative checkpoints (posture, timing) not lab torque numbers in consumer UI.

3. SIDE-FOOT PASS (COACHING AUTHORITY — B; BIOMECH B+ LIMITED)

FIFA Grassroots “basic techniques” and futsal manuals emphasize:
  - Inside-of-foot contact for short passing; body over ball; follow-through toward target.
  - Futsal: more touches, shorter passes, technical development in constrained space.
  URLs:
    - FIFA Grassroots Manual (PDF mirror on member association sites; verify against FIFA hub):
      https://cdn1.sportngin.com/attachments/document/8546-2348967/FIFA_grassroots_en.pdf
    - FIFA Futsal Coaching Manual EN:
      https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/media/native/community-area-document/resources/futsal/FIFA_GFD_Futsal_Coaching_Manual_EN.pdf
  Authority: B (FIFA GFD)
  Biomechanics: side-foot = lower peak foot speed, greater accuracy emphasis (Kellis review);
  build Biomechanics/001 as qualitative contrast to instep, not duplicate instep angles.

  Key coaching emphasis: knee-over-toe, soft landings, straight leg alignment (Training/001 cross-link).

9. COMMON MECHANICS THEMES → BODY SIGNALS (SEEDS FOR Findings/ — NOT PRESCRIPTIVE)

| Theme | Body-measurable signal (pose/OpenSim) | Coaching direction | Sources |
|-------|--------------------------------------|--------------------|---------|
| Trunk too upright at strike | Low trunk flexion vs run-up | Use hip flexion + arm counterbalance cues; accuracy vs power tradeoff | Kellis PMC3786235; 2021 SR |
| Over-lean / loss of balance post strike | Excessive trunk flexion; slow recovery step | Core control; decel through follow | Shan 2011; Kellis |
| Plant foot too open | Support toe angle / pelvis opens early | Align plant beside ball; stabilize before backswing | Lees 2010 themes; coaching consensus |
| “All leg” swing | Arms static; low contralateral arm drive | Use arm swing for balance/timing | Shan 2011 whole-body |
| Backswing too short | Knee flex amplitude low before forward swing | Increase leg cocking via hip ext mobility drills | Kellis |
| Forward swing only from knee | Hip extension lag; shank peak before thigh | Proximal-to-distal timing drills | Kellis |
| Side-foot: body behind ball | Trunk behind CoM at contact_proxy | Pass on ground; lean over ball | FIFA grassroots techniques |
| Max reps youth | N/A (training load) | Cap volley/max shots; prioritize variety | FA DNA; FIFA 11+ |

Do **not** publish single “correct” knee/hip angles as medical or performance guarantees.

10. SUGGESTED SERIES / CHECKPOINTS (ALIGN brain_plan Quality/)

From literature + brain_plan:
  - approach: CoM velocity toward kick direction, step cadence
  - plant: support knee flexion, pelvis orientation, CoM deceleration
  - backswing: kicking-side knee flexion peak, hip extension
  - forward_swing: kicking-side knee extension rate, foot speed rise
  - contact_proxy: peak ||v(kicking_ankle)||
  - follow: trunk angle recovery, balance (single-leg stability duration)

Compare instep vs side-foot: expect lower peak foot speed and less trunk range for side-foot passes
(Kellis accuracy vs power; FIFA technique descriptions).
