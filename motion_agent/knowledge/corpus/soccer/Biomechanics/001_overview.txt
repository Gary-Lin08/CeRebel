# Soccer Kicking — Biomechanics Overview (open literature summary)

Authority tags: A+ federation | A clinical/consensus | B+ systematic reviews | B coaching science | C not used for findings.

This document summarizes **relationships** visible on a body model. It does not prescribe medical treatment or
individual angle prescriptions.

---

## 1. Kinetic chain (proximal to distal)

Published kicking analyses commonly describe energy transfer from support foot through pelvis and trunk to the
kicking leg:

1. **Support leg** grounds reaction forces; stiffness and orientation affect pelvis stability (B+).
2. **Pelvis rotation** toward the kicking side precedes or overlaps peak trunk rotation (B+).
3. **Trunk tilt and rotation** contribute to foot speed and variability in strike outcomes (B+).
4. **Kicking hip** flexes/extends with knee flexion during cocking, then rapid knee extension and ankle plantar
   flexion approaching peak foot speed (B+).
5. **Follow-through** involves hip flexion across the body and controlled deceleration — hamstring and hip flexor
   demand depends on speed and style (A/B+ for load awareness, not individual risk prediction).

CeRebel v1 measures joint positions and segment speeds, **not** ground reaction forces or joint torques unless
future force plates integrate.

## 3. Support foot and pelvis

Reviews of soccer kicking report that support-foot placement relative to the ball influences pelvis rotation and
the path of the kicking foot. Coaches teach planting ** beside** the ball with orientation that allows rotation
toward target. Studies vary by skill level and kick type (instep vs side-foot).

- **Authority:** B+ (Lees & Nolan 1998 review; subsequent instep kick kinematics papers).
- **Youth:** Smaller athletes use shorter relative approach and plant distances; absolute adult templates do not apply.

**Product implication:** cp_hip_open_proxy and plant distance checkpoints are conservative heuristics for monocular pose.

---

## 4. Trunk orientation and foot speed

Forward trunk lean at ball contact (or near peak foot speed) appears in many skilled instep kick profiles compared
with excessive backward lean associated with high ball flight in coaching models. Meta-analytic summaries caution
that laboratory setups differ from match constraints.

- **Adult bands (qualitative):** Visible forward lean at peak commonly coached; exact degrees vary by camera setup.
- **Youth bands:** Less aggressive forward lean required for skill development; balance priority (B, NGB curricula).

**Do not** state “X degrees prevents injury” — literature links mechanics to performance variability and joint
loading patterns in group data only.

## 5. Joint angle profiles (instep kick, literature ranges)

Reported **group means** from motion capture studies (not CeRebel norms):

| Joint / segment | Typical observation near contact (adult skilled cohorts) | Authority |
|-----------------|----------------------------------------------------------|-----------|
| Kicking knee flex at end of backswing | Often substantial flexion before rapid extension | B+ |
| Kicking knee at contact | Moving toward extension; not full lock required | B+ |
| Support knee | Moderate flexion; less increase through strike in skilled patterns | B+ |
| Hip flexion/extension kicking side | Rapid extension phase into contact | B+ |

Youth athletes show lower absolute foot speeds and sometimes shorter backswing phases — separate coaching bands in Quality docs.

---

## 6. Timing: plant to peak foot speed

Time from support foot stabilization to peak foot speed is frequently on the order of **~100–200 ms** in controlled
lab kicks for adults (varies by approach speed and intent). Product checkpoint cp_plant_timing uses ~80 ms minimum
at 30 fps as a B+ heuristic for detector alignment.

Penalty kicks and curved approaches alter timing; phase model still applies with wider windows.

## 7. Side-foot pass vs instep drive

| Feature | Instep drive (power) | Side-foot pass |
|---------|----------------------|----------------|
| Foot speed peak | Higher | Moderate |
| Trunk lean | Often more forward | More upright acceptable |
| Hip rotation | Larger | Reduced |
| Plant distance | Mid band of cp_plant_distance | Often shorter |

Intent selection in product UI should gate checkpoint strictness.

---

## 8. Load and injury context (careful wording)

Hamstring, groin, and hip flexor strains occur in soccer from sprinting, kicking, and deceleration. Biomechanics
literature discusses muscle activity during kicking and deceleration; **no finding in this pack diagnoses injury risk**.

- Follow federation/medical consensus on weekly load, especially U13–U17 growth phases (A, general sports medicine).
- Maximal repeated instep striking in fatigue states increases variability — coach-managed volume (B+).
